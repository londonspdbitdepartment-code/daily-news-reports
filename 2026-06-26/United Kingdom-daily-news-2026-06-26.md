### HM Treasury Publishes June 2026 Forecast Compendium as Flash PMI Slips Into Contraction

HM Treasury released its monthly "Forecasts for the UK Economy" compendium for June 2026 on 26 June, aggregating independent growth and fiscal projections from a panel of City and academic economists. The release landed against a darkening near-term backdrop: the UK composite PMI flash reading fell to 49.4 in June, published 23 June, the first sub-50 print in over a year and a 14-month low.

Services did the damage. The services activity index dropped to 48.7 against the 50.0 the market expected — a multi-year low — as new business volumes fell faster and firms cut headcount. A reading below 50 signals contraction; the flash data point to a second straight month of output decline and a flat second quarter. Manufacturing held up better by comparison.

The Treasury's central-tendency figures cover GDP growth for the current and following year, CPI inflation, the current-account balance, and unemployment and wage growth. The compendium frames a Bank of England still boxed in: inflation remains above the 2% target, and the MPC held Bank Rate at 4.25% at its June meeting rather than cutting into a softening economy. Price pressures stayed elevated as companies cited an energy shock and supply squeeze tied to Middle East conflict.

### UK Raises Heat-Pump Grant to £9,000 for 1.7 Million Oil and LPG Homes

The government will lift the Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant 20%, from £7,500 to £9,000, for roughly 1.7 million homes in England and Wales running on heating oil or LPG, ministers announced on 26 June. The higher grant takes effect on 21 July 2026 and becomes the most generous level of support for switching to a heat pump anywhere in England and Wales.

Around 200,000 off-grid households will receive information leaflets. The targeting is deliberate: off-gas-grid homes — concentrated in rural areas from Cornwall to Lincolnshire — cannot shelter under the domestic energy price cap and are exposed directly to global oil markets through periodic deliveries. Energy minister Martin McCluskey cited Middle East-driven oil price volatility as a driver. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme launched in May 2022 and runs as part of the Warm Homes Plan; the £7,500 rate had applied across most boiler replacements until this carve-out.

The grant uplift came bundled with a £100 million boost to the Social Housing Fund for solar power and an expansion of Great British Energy's school solar scheme.

## 2. Politics & Diplomacy

### UK Issues Rare Red Heat Warning as Record June Heat Triggers COBR Response

The UK issued a rare red heat warning on 26 June as temperatures across southern and central England were forecast to exceed 40°C, closing on the all-time record of 40.3°C set in Lincolnshire in July 2022. The Met Office red warning — its highest level, signalling "danger to life" for everyone, not only the vulnerable — covered England from the South West to East Anglia, with amber warnings reaching into the Midlands and Wales.

Ministers convened emergency COBR meetings and coordinated with NHS England, Transport for London and National Rail. Network operators imposed train speed restrictions to stop tracks buckling in the heat, and schools in affected areas were permitted to send pupils home early. Forecasters flagged that high humidity made this episode more oppressive than July 2022, when dew points were lower, with concern over consecutive nights staying above 20°C.

The political sting: the government's national heat resilience plan, consulted on in 2024, had still not been enacted when the warning was issued.

### UK Launches First-Ever Climate Security Taskforce Co-Chaired by Two Ministers

The UK government launched its first Climate Security Taskforce on 26 June, co-chaired by Climate Minister Katie White and Security Minister Dame Angela Eagle, bringing together eight leading security, military and academic specialists — among them Prof. Tim Benton of Leeds and General Richard Nugee of the MOD.

The taskforce will pinpoint gaps in UK preparedness and assess how climate and nature threats feed into national security: cross-border migration, food and water access, energy systems, supply chains and financial stability. "The climate crisis is a growing threat to our national security," Eagle said in framing the body's remit. It launched during London Climate Action Week, running 22–28 June, where White appeared alongside the EU's climate envoy and a UN special rapporteur on a defence-and-security panel.

The move folds climate risk into the formal national-security machinery rather than leaving it to environmental departments alone.

### New Anglesey Nuclear Station Officially Named 'Gwyndod' by Community Panel

Great British Energy – Nuclear (GBE-N) unveiled the community-chosen name "Gwyndod Power Station" on 26 June for the new nuclear facility on Anglesey's Wylfa site, selected from hundreds of submissions. A panel of young Ynys Môn residents made the choice, chaired by Ynys Môn MP Llinos Medi and Secretary of State for Wales Jo Stevens.

The naming caps a fast-moving year for the site. Wylfa was confirmed in November 2025 as the launch location for the UK's first small modular reactor fleet, and in April 2026 GBE-N and Rolls-Royce SMR signed a contract clearing site activity to begin. The initial project covers three reactors, with the decommissioned site able to hold up to eight; £2.6 billion was allocated in the 2025 Spending Review to enable delivery, and the reactors are expected to feed the grid from the mid-2030s.

## 4. Society & Livelihood

### Scientists Say Europe's Record June 2026 Heatwave Was 'Virtually Impossible' Without Climate Change

The June 2026 European heatwave would have been "virtually impossible" without human-caused climate change, the World Weather Attribution (WWA) group concluded in a rapid attribution analysis published 26 June. Climate change increased the likelihood of such an event by a factor of hundreds.

The team modelled temperature anomalies across Western and Central Europe — the UK, France, Germany, the Netherlands and Iberia — and found the event exceeded the 2003 and 2019 European heat records in both extent and intensity. For the UK, temperatures over 40°C, first crossed in July 2022, were again observed or forecast on 26 June.

WWA runs these analyses within days of an event precisely to counter the framing of extreme heat as random misfortune. The finding lands while a red Met Office warning is live across England, sharpening the link between the day's emergency and the longer warming trend.

### NHS Hospitals Declare Critical Incidents as Heatwave Overwhelms Emergency Services

Multiple NHS hospital trusts across England declared critical incidents on 26 June as extreme heat overwhelmed emergency departments. Ambulance services in the South East, South West and East of England ran at or above full stretch, with Category 2 response times exceeding two hours in some areas. Ward temperatures in older buildings reportedly topped 35°C.

NHS England activated its heat health action plan, but leaders warned it had not been updated since 2023. NHS Confederation chief executive Matthew Taylor called for emergency investment in hospital ventilation and cooling. The BMA put hard numbers on the underlying problem: the NHS estates maintenance backlog had passed £11 billion, leaving wards in buildings never designed for 40°C summers.

A critical incident is a formal declaration that demand has outstripped a trust's ability to deliver safe care — used to trigger mutual aid and divert resources — and seeing several issued at once on a single hot day signals system-wide strain rather than isolated pressure.

### BMA Warns Outdated NHS Buildings Put Patients and Staff at Serious Risk

Over half of NHS hospital buildings in England were built before 1985 without modern ventilation, the BMA warned on 26 June, putting patients and staff at serious risk as the heatwave drove indoor temperatures up. The association urged the government to accelerate the New Hospital Programme, noting the NHS maintenance backlog had grown to about £11.6 billion.

"Every heatwave like this one will be worse until we fix the buildings. This is not an act of God — it is a policy failure," BMA council chair Professor Philip Banfield said.

The intervention reframes extreme heat as an estates and capital question, not a weather one: the issue is not the temperature outside but the cost of cooling — or replacing — a hospital stock built for a cooler climate.

### UKHSA Extends Red Heat-Health Alert Across Six English Regions Until Friday Night

UKHSA extended red heat-health alerts for six English regions until 11pm on 26 June — only the second time a red alert has been issued since the scheme began, the first being July 2022. The alert covered the South West, South East, London, East of England, West Midlands and East Midlands, with all remaining English regions under amber.

Temperatures peaked at 36.7°C in Merryfield, Somerset on 25 June, breaking the UK June record. A red heat-health alert signals that heat effects will be felt across the whole population and the entire health and social-care system, not just among the clinically vulnerable — the trigger NHS trusts and care providers use to escalate their own responses.
