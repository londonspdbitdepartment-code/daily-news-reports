### South Korea's June consumer prices jump 3.2 percent to a 30-month high as fuel costs surge

South Korea's consumer price index rose 3.2 percent year-on-year in June 2026, the steepest increase since December 2023 and a step up from May's 3.1 percent. The driver was energy: fuel prices surged 24.7 percent year-on-year, with gasoline up 23.1 percent and diesel up 33.7 percent, together contributing 0.93 percentage points to headline inflation. That spike traces back to Middle East conflict that pushed crude higher through the spring. Industrial product prices rose 4.4 percent and agricultural and fishery goods 3.2 percent, while service prices advanced 2.6 percent. Core CPI, stripping out volatile food and energy, rose 2.5 percent. On a monthly basis prices ticked up just 0.1 percent.

Bank of Korea Deputy Governor Lee Ji-ho said: "Consumer prices in June expanded further from May as petroleum product prices remained elevated and the increase in agricultural product prices accelerated." At its May 28 meeting the central bank's monetary policy board voted 5-2 to hold the benchmark rate at 2.50 percent, and markets now expect a move at or before the July 16 policy meeting — a rate hike being weighed even as growth stays soft, an awkward bind for policymakers. First Vice Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il set the near-term aim of keeping inflation around 3 percent in the second half of 2026. On July 2, Prime Minister Han Seong-sook made the point bluntly: "More than anything else, we should make it our highest priority to concentrate on managing prices."

### Lawmakers finally enter a blockaded Seoul gymnasium storing 2.47 million ballots, flagging surveillance gaps

A parliamentary special committee investigating the June 3, 2026 local election ballot shortages conducted its first on-site inspection of the Olympic Park Handball Gymnasium in Songpa-gu, Seoul on July 2, 2026 — the committee's first access in 27 days. The facility has been blockaded by protesters since June 5, when ballot papers ran out at 50 of South Korea's roughly 14,300 polling stations and voting was temporarily halted at 22 stations, 19 of them in the wealthy Songpa and Gangnam districts. The shortage stemmed from the National Election Commission printing far fewer election-day ballots than the electorate, betting on high early turnout — a miscalculation that forced the NEC chairman to resign and spawned weeks of protests demanding a rerun.

The 40-minute inspection focused on the security of roughly 380 ballot boxes holding 2.47 million ballots from Songpa-gu. Police deployed roughly 2,000 personnel as scuffles broke out among protesters outside. Democratic Party Rep. Kim Yong-man was blunt about the storage: "It is absurd for the NEC to store the ballots in such a place. The boxes are not being stored securely." People Power Party Rep. Joo Jin-woo urged verifying camera blind spots before recommending any relocation. No evidence of deliberate manipulation has emerged, but the surveillance gaps identified inside are likely to fuel the opposition's demands for a fuller audit.

### Heavy water leaks at the Wolsong-4 reactor, but 208 kg stays contained with no radiation detected

Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power notified the Nuclear Safety and Security Commission at approximately 2:26 p.m. on Wednesday, July 1, 2026 of a heavy water leak at the Wolsong-4 CANDU-type nuclear reactor in Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province. Approximately 208 kilograms (458 pounds) of heavy water had leaked as of 9 p.m. Wednesday, all of it contained within the facility with no external leakage. KHNP shut down the associated pumps, and the reactor had in any case been offline for scheduled maintenance when the leak occurred.

An NSSC official confirmed the key reassurance: "No irregularities in external radiation levels have been detected." In CANDU reactors heavy water — water enriched with the hydrogen isotope deuterium — serves as both moderator and coolant and is not radioactive under normal operating conditions, which limits the health risk from a contained spill. The NSSC said it will dispatch expert teams for an on-site inspection to determine the cause. The incident revives scrutiny of the aging Wolsong complex, which has drawn repeated safety questions in recent years.

### South Korea eases K-Tech Pass visa screening for foreign tech workers, scrapping the Korean-language test

The South Korean government on July 2, 2026 expanded the K-Tech Pass visa program with a dual-track screening system designed to draw more foreign technology talent as the population shrinks. Track one combines 65 points for quantitative criteria with 35 points for qualitative assessment, and small and medium-sized enterprises receive an additional 10 bonus points to help them compete with conglomerates for scarce specialists. Track two links to overseas scholar-recruitment programs. In the most consequential change, the Korean-language requirement — previously TOPIK Level 1 — has been eliminated, lowering a barrier that had deterred many mid-career applicants.

The benefits package is aimed squarely at making relocation attractive: a 50 percent income-tax reduction for up to 10 years, special international-school admission quotas for applicants' children, and Jeonse deposit-loan limits raised from 200 million won to 500 million won, alongside settlement assistance. The overhaul reflects a demographic squeeze — a falling working-age population and intensifying global competition for AI and semiconductor engineers — that Korea is trying to offset through immigration.

### Samsung and SK hynix narrow the Gwangju fab site search to two candidates under the W800 trillion chip plan

Samsung Electronics and SK hynix on July 2, 2026 narrowed their site search for new Gwangju-area chip fabrication plants to two candidates under the government's 800 trillion won ($514.7 billion) semiconductor ecosystem plan, announced June 29. The first candidate is Cheomdan Industrial District 3 within Gwangju Innopolis, a 3.63 million square meter site close to an announced 1 trillion won Amkor Technology packaging investment that would give a new fab a ready supply-chain neighbor; site development there is due to finish in September 2027, allowing construction to start soon after. ### US-Iran talks in Doha end without a Strait of Hormuz breakthrough, with the next round awaiting Khamenei's funeral

Two days of indirect US-Iran technical talks in Doha concluded on July 2, 2026 with no breakthrough, focused on Strait of Hormuz maritime traffic and the unfreezing of Iranian funds under an interim agreement announced two weeks earlier. Qatar's Foreign Ministry, acting as mediator, cited "positive progress" on the memorandum's outcomes but pointed to no concrete deal. High-level US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff did not attend; Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi led its delegation, and US Vice President JD Vance signaled that the nuclear program would be addressed later. The stakes are direct for South Korea. The Strait of Hormuz handles approximately one-fifth of global oil and LNG trade, and elevated fuel prices drove a 24.7 percent year-on-year rise in Korean fuel costs, contributing 0.93 percentage points to June CPI — the 30-month high of 3.2 percent that now dominates domestic policy. Iran plans to begin assessing tolls on shipping through the strait from mid-August, a move that could keep energy import costs — and Korean inflation — under pressure.
