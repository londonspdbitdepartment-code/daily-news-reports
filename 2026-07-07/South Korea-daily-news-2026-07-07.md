### Global banks lift South Korea 2026 growth consensus to 3% as AI chip exports surge

The average 2026 growth forecast among eight major global investment banks reached 3.0 percent as of end-June, the first time their consensus hit that mark. The upgrades were led by JP Morgan, which lifted its call to 3.7 percent from 3.0 percent, and Citibank, which moved to 3.5 percent from 3.0 percent. Goldman Sachs raised its forecast to 2.7 percent, Barclays to 2.7 percent, and HSBC to 2.8 percent. Bank of America held at 3.1 percent, UBS at 2.8 percent, and Nomura, the most cautious, at 2.4 percent. Outside the bank consensus, Capital Economics projects 4.0 percent and Korean Re 4.1 percent.

The revisions track a violent turn in the export data. June exports hit a record $102.25 billion, up 70.9 percent year-on-year, with semiconductor shipments nearly tripling to $44.82 billion as demand for AI memory chips broadened well beyond high-bandwidth memory. The current account surplus forecast climbed to 14 percent of nominal GDP at end-June, from 10.8 percent in May.

Bank of Korea Governor Shin Hyun-song signaled the central bank will follow the private sector. "The central bank's forecast would be revised upward 'mechanically' from the current 2.6 percent," he said, pointing to an update in August. The BOK has spent much of the past two years lagging market optimism about the chip cycle; a move toward 3 percent would mark its first upgrade of this magnitude in the current AI-driven upswing.

### KOSPI triggers year's sixth circuit breaker, closes down 5.82% despite Samsung record earnings

The KOSPI plunged 5.82 percent to close at 7,582.69 on July 7, even as Samsung Electronics reported record second-quarter operating profit of 89.4 trillion won hours earlier. The index opened at 7,919.20, already down 132.13 points, and the slide accelerated through the session. Korea Exchange activated a sell-side sidecar at 10:23 a.m., suspending program sells for five minutes, then triggered a market-wide circuit breaker at 1:51 p.m. that halted trading for 20 minutes. It was the sixth circuit breaker of 2026, on a market that has grown extraordinarily volatile after a year of chip-led gains punctuated by sharp drops in March and June.

The selling was driven from abroad. Foreign investors dumped a net 3.75 trillion won, while retail investors net bought 3.43 trillion won and institutions added a token 23.9 billion won. The heavyweights led the fall: Samsung Electronics dropped 6.92 percent to 296,000 won, SK hynix fell 6.53 percent to 2.19 million won, and SK Square tumbled 10.77 percent to 1.334 million won. Hanwha Ocean cratered 22.65 percent after Canada picked a German rival for a submarine contract. A handful of defensive names bucked the rout, with Samyang Foods up 11.29 percent and KT&G gaining 6.26 percent.

Analysts framed the drop as profit-taking rather than a break in the trend. "For the market's upward trajectory to break, concerns that earnings have peaked would likely need to become much more immediate," said Han Ji-young, an analyst at Kiwoom Securities.

### Seven in ten Koreans back an EU-style economic community with Japan, KCCI survey finds

Some 69.8 percent of Koreans support building an EU-style economic bloc with Japan, either immediately or over the medium term, according to a Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry survey conducted by Hankook Research with 500 respondents in each country. On the Japanese side, 59.8 percent agreed. Support among Japanese who had visited Korea in the past five years ran far higher, at 74.5 percent, against 45.4 percent among those who had not.

The proposal that drew most attention is a "Korea-Japan Schengen" — a mutual visa-recognition scheme that would let travelers move between the two countries on national ID cards rather than passports. Some 60.4 percent of Koreans favored ID-card travel; 44.8 percent of Japanese concurred. The gap matters given that only 17.5 percent of Japanese held passports as of February 2025. The KCCI estimates the scheme could attract 1.84 million additional annual visitors to Korea. Broader tourism cooperation drew 76.8 percent Korean and 58 percent Japanese backing.

The KCCI, led by SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won, has floated a Korea-Japan economic community repeatedly as a hedge against a fracturing global trading order; a combined Korea-Japan economy would rank behind only the United States, the EU and China. National Assembly Speaker Cho Jeong-sik joined a related forum alongside Chey.

### Lawmakers demand delisting of Samsung and SK Hynix leveraged ETFs as losses hit 35.9%

All 14 single-stock leveraged ETFs tied to Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix posted negative one-month returns by July 7, with the worst losses reaching 35.9 percent — even as the underlying shares rose over the same span. The products, which launched May 27 and aim to deliver twice a stock's daily move, decay when prices swing, so heavy volatility eroded value regardless of direction. Some 212 trillion won ($139 billion) had churned through leveraged funds since the debut, and the KOSPI's daily trading range widened sharply after their arrival.

The political backlash sharpened on July 7. Rep. Ahn Cheol-soo of the People Power Party demanded forced delistings and the dismissal of the Financial Services Commission chairman and the Financial Supervisory Service governor. "KOSPI has devolved into a casino," he said. FSS Governor Lee Chan-jin, who had voiced regret in June, conceded again: "We rushed the process at the time." The Bank of Korea had earlier warned the products could draw excessive money into a handful of stocks and amplify volatility. A forced delisting is viewed as unlikely, given the absence of a clear legal basis.

### Lee vows continued K-defense support after Hanwha Ocean loses $39 billion Canada submarine bid

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney announced on July 6 that Germany's ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems had been selected as preferred bidder for the Canadian Patrol Submarine Project — up to 12 diesel-electric submarines plus 30 years of maintenance, worth roughly 60 trillion won ($39.24 billion). "This was a difficult, close decision between two highly qualified suppliers," Carney said. Hanwha Ocean, bidding in consortium with HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and offering its KSS-III Batch II design, lost out. Its shares fell 22.65 percent on July 7.

Canada leaned toward interoperability: the German 212CD platform is shared with Norway, optimized for Arctic waters, and already in service across much of NATO. Hanwha Ocean was blunt about the obstacle, saying it "was unable to overcome the wall of the NATO alliance." The loss marks a second setback against TKMS after Korea withdrew from India's Project-75I program, which the German firm went on to win.

President Lee sought to steady the sector. "Our submarines have proven their outstanding performance and technological capabilities by competing with the world's leading submarine builders," he said. The Defense Acquisition Program Administration attributed the outcome partly to "unfavorable strategic conditions." Canada retains the right to negotiate with Hanwha Ocean as a backup should talks with TKMS collapse.

### South Korea's anti-disinformation law takes effect, imposing 5x punitive damages and platform duties

South Korea began enforcing its revised Information and Communications Network Act on July 7, letting courts impose punitive damages of up to five times proven losses on news organizations and social-media influencers who distribute false, illegal or manipulated content for profit. Large platforms with more than 1 million daily users must remove flagged content or suspend accounts. Accounts with 100,000 or more subscribers or 100,000-plus average monthly views count as "major information publishers," and repeat offenders face fines reaching 1 billion won ($656,000).

Backlash has been fierce. A Korea JoongAng Daily analysis of 10,057 posts across DC Inside, FM Korea and Ruliweb found 63.2 percent expressed concern and only 1.1 percent — 109 posts — were supportive; among users, 351 posts discussed political action, 121 discussed moving to overseas platforms and 57 discussed VPNs. A public petition against the law gathered more than 140,000 signatures, and the People Power Party filed a constitutional challenge.

Press and rights groups warn the vaguely drafted statute could chill critical reporting on officials and big business. The Journalists Association of Korea cautioned it could produce an "unavoidable chilling effect," and the Seoul Foreign Correspondents' Club raised freedom-of-expression concerns; the US State Department and UNESCO had criticized the bill during passage. Ruling Democratic Party leader Han Byung-do defended it: "Those spreading malicious false information have caused extensive damage while reaping unfair profits. The revised law is the minimum institutional tool needed to prevent the production and distribution of such false and manipulated information." The bill passed the National Assembly in December 2025 amid opposition boycotts.

### Warming seas drive fourfold jump in shark sightings and a jellyfish surge at Korean beaches

As of June 24, 46 large sharks had been reported in Korean waters — nearly four times the 12 recorded in the same period of 2025. Sightings clustered near Anmok and Gyeongpo beaches in Gangneung, Gangwon Province. Alongside them, Nomura's jellyfish appearance rate in Korean waters climbed from 2.9 percent on June 18 to 11.4 percent by early July. Around Jeju Island the 2026 rate reached 80 percent, up from 36 percent in 2016. The species can grow to 2 meters across and weigh up to 200 kg, clogging fishing nets and damaging catches.

The National Institute of Fisheries Science attributes the surges to warming ocean temperatures. "Rising water temperatures are driving a sharp increase in venomous jellyfish, posing growing risks" to swimmers, fishermen and coastal industries, it said. Coastal authorities have installed offshore safety nets and issued advisories ahead of the summer season.

### HD Hyundai Heavy Industries to refund all meal costs collected from foreign workers since 2023

HD Hyundai Heavy Industries said it will fully reimburse meal costs deducted from foreign workers, averaging about 7 million won ($4,600) per worker and covering deductions back to 2023. Multilingual consultation sessions ran over three days starting June 30.

### New FSC rules on subsidiary IPOs take effect, leaving HD Hyundai, SK, CJ and Lotte in a dilemma

Korea's Financial Services Commission and Korea Exchange introduced rules on July 7 requiring listed companies to win shareholder approval before separately listing spun-off subsidiaries. Parent boards must set up independent special committees of at least three members, evaluate the impact on shareholders, propose protections such as higher dividends or treasury-share cancellations, formally vote and disclose the process. A "3 percent cap rule" limits controlling shareholders to 3 percent of the vote, and proposals must win a majority of votes cast plus at least one-quarter of all eligible voting rights. Violations carry fines up to 1 billion won ($653,000) and a one-day trading suspension.

The rules target the "Korea discount," the persistent valuation gap partly blamed on parent companies floating subsidiaries at minority shareholders' expense; listed subsidiaries make up 11.2 percent of Korea's market cap, versus 4.0 percent in Japan, 2.7 percent in Taiwan and just 0.05 percent in the US. Affected plans include HD Hyundai Robotics, where preparations have stalled; CJ Olive Young, now likely to merge with parent CJ Group; SK ecoplant, repurchasing pre-IPO investor stakes; and Lotte Biologics. The regime tightens rules on split-off subsidiaries while easing them for M&A-acquired and newly created units.

"The real test will be whether these committees can allow an objective forum where companies and shareholders can engage in meaningful dialogue," said Yang Jun-seok, an economics professor at Catholic University of Korea.

### Incheon Airport passes 1 billion cumulative passengers in 25 years, fastest among global hubs

Incheon International Airport surpassed 1 billion cumulative passengers in July 2026, 25 years and 3 months after opening on March 29, 2001 — 9,232 days, at a pace of 108,000 passengers a day, 4,513 an hour and 75 a minute. The one-billionth traveler was Hara Ayaka, a Japanese national departing for Tokyo on Korean Air flight KE713, who received a commemorative plaque and airline tickets.

No global hub has reached the milestone faster: Munich took 34 years, Singapore Changi 35, Narita 39 and Dubai more than 58. Incheon now ranks third worldwide in international passenger traffic, with 74.1 million international passengers in 2025, serving 183 cities in 53 countries through 101 airlines, and handling roughly 99 percent of Korea's semiconductor exports by value. Its fourth-phase expansion, completed in 2024, lifted annual capacity to 106 million passengers.

"Incheon Airport has grown into a global gateway thanks to the support of the government, the public and our airport workers," said acting president Kim Bum-ho. The airport is targeting 30 million foreign visitors a year, up from 18.94 million in 2025.

### South Korea's twin pandas mark their third birthday at Everland, likely their last in Korea

Giant pandas Rui Bao and Hui Bao, born July 7, 2023, to parents Ai Bao and Le Bao at Everland's Panda World in Yongin, Gyeonggi, celebrated their third birthday on July 7, 2026. Each now weighs more than 80 kg. The celebration is expected to be their last in Korea before they leave for China, with relocation anticipated as early as winter 2026.

Under international agreements, giant pandas born outside China must return before reaching breeding age at four years old. Their older sister, Fu Bao — a national sensation during her time in Korea — departed for China in April 2024.
