### China breaks step with global markets as yuan and bonds draw foreign inflows

China's 10-year sovereign yield has fallen nearly 10 basis points to 1.73% since the Middle East conflict began at the end of February 2026, even as 10-year US yields rose 51 basis points over the same stretch — a divergence that has turned the country's bond market into the world's strongest performer of the period. The yuan has advanced 5.4% against the dollar over 12 months, reaching a 3.5-year high of 6.7522 per dollar, the only major currency to have climbed against the greenback in that window. Mainland blue-chip stocks rose approximately 11% in dollar terms in the first half of 2026.

The money is following. China's bond market logged net foreign inflows for the first time in more than a year in May 2026, and foreign holdings of onshore A-shares increased from approximately 3.67 trillion yuan at year-end 2025 to over 4 trillion yuan. Christopher Hamilton at Invesco said, "The role of China in portfolios is evolving from a simple emerging-market growth allocation toward a more nuanced source of diversification." BNY Asia-Pacific macro strategist Wee Khoon Chong attributed the move to fundamentals: "There has been renewed demand for China bonds, which we believe was driven by relative safety and low volatility."

The shift is being framed as structural rather than tactical. Phillip Wool, head of portfolio management at Rayliant, said, "We've long seen China's market, especially onshore-listed China A-shares, as a rare source of diversification. Now, in addition, you've got an actual economic decoupling that's happening." CEIBS Finance Institute director Liu Gongrun put it more bluntly: "Chinese asset allocation is no longer determined by valuations, trading sentiment, or Fed rate changes." With the benchmark 7-day reverse repo rate at 1.40% and the CSI 300 trading near 4,842, Goldman Sachs holds a 12-month CSI 300 target of 5,300.

### PBOC's Pan lifts Southbound Bond Connect quota to 800bn yuan as Hong Kong launches gold central clearing

PBOC Governor Pan Gongsheng told the FIC & Bond Connect Summit in Hong Kong on July 7, 2026 that the annual net investment quota under Southbound Bond Connect will expand from 500 billion to 800 billion yuan (approx US$117.8 billion), widening the channel through which mainland investors buy offshore debt. Eligible products expand to include Hong Kong dollar bonds and related yuan-denominated assets, and the scope now extends to Macau's bond market. Pan said China's national foreign-exchange reserves would increase their allocation to Hong Kong financial markets across asset classes.

Alongside the quota move, the RMB Business Facility will more than double to 500 billion yuan ($74 billion), giving local banks greater renminbi access via HKMA loans — a step aimed at deepening offshore yuan liquidity. Separately, Hong Kong Chief Executive John Lee announced a pilot central clearing system for gold under the government-owned Hong Kong Precious Metals Central Clearing Limited, the first phase of "Delivery Connect," developed with the Shanghai Gold Exchange to link the two cities' physical gold liquidity pools. A new gold price ticker, HAU, developed with Bloomberg, was introduced.

### Tencent executes $1.5B block sale of Kuaishou stake, cutting below 10% threshold amid AI pivot

Tencent Holdings sold approximately 272.95 million Class B Kuaishou shares at HK$43.25 in an off-market block trade around July 6-7, 2026, netting roughly HK$12.56 billion (US$1.5 billion) — the biggest such block trade in Hong Kong in 2026. ## 2. Politics & Diplomacy

### China test-fires submarine-launched ballistic missile into South Pacific

China's PLA Navy test-launched a long-range ballistic missile from a nuclear-powered submarine in the South Pacific at 12:01 p.m. local time on July 7, 2026. Analysts identified the weapon as a JL-3 SLBM, with a range exceeding 10,000 km, fired from a Type 094 (Jin-class) submarine and carrying a non-nuclear dummy warhead that impacted within the South Pacific Nuclear Free Zone established under the 1986 Treaty of Rarotonga. China acknowledged the launch through Xinhua and the Ministry of Defense, calling it "part of routine annual training, complied with international law and practice and was not directed against any country or target."

Beijing notified Australia, Japan, and New Zealand in advance but not the United States, and the timing coincided with Australia and Fiji signing a bilateral mutual-defense treaty, the "Ocean of Peace." Australian Foreign Minister Penny Wong called the test "destabilizing to the region." New Zealand Foreign Minister Winston Peters noted, "China carried out the test within hours of informing us." US State Department spokesperson Thomas Pigott said, "Beijing's rapid and opaque nuclear weapons buildup is of great concern."

Defense analyst Lyle Morris described it as the first publicly acknowledged test demonstrating China's submarine-based nuclear deterrent reaching this far into the Pacific. China operates six Type 094 submarines, and the launch follows the country's September 2024 DF-31B ICBM test into the same region.

Bloomberg's July 7 analysis frames Xi Jinping's release, at Donald Trump's personal request, of Pastor Ezra Jin Mingri — leader of the unregistered Zion Church — as a low-cost gesture that showcases warming US-China relations and clears the path toward a September 2026 summit. The diplomatic dividend is the larger story. Trump has invited Xi to the White House around September 24, 2026, coinciding with the UN General Assembly, and the pastor's release removes a persistent human-rights irritant ahead of that meeting. Al Jazeera reported the gesture landed as both governments sought to stabilize ties strained by tariffs and technology controls.

### Wang Yi in Oslo: Norway urges China to press Russia toward Ukraine talks

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi completed the Oslo leg of his week-long Nordic tour on July 7, 2026, meeting Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store and Foreign Minister Espen Barth Eide after stops that included Denmark on July 3, Sweden, and Finland — the most comprehensive Chinese foreign-ministerial engagement with the Nordic region in years. Ukraine dominated the Oslo discussions. Store told reporters, "China is probably the country with the best and most direct access to the Russian leadership. Store warned that a "cheap ceasefire forcing Ukraine to give up land" would create an "expensive peace for Europe." Wang's consistent message across the tour was that China and Europe are "partners, not rivals." The Oslo talks ran concurrently with the NATO summit in Ankara on July 7-8, where Secretary General Mark Rutte highlighted China's deepening defense-industrial cooperation with Russia as a core alliance concern.

### Chinese companies accelerate shift from Nvidia to domestic AI chip suppliers, Bloomberg Intelligence survey finds

A Bloomberg Intelligence survey released Tuesday, July 7, 2026 found Chinese companies moving decisively from Nvidia accelerators to domestic silicon. Executives said they will allocate 46% of their AI-accelerator budget to domestic products over the next 12 months, up from 30% currently, and 80% reported that total infrastructure spending is over-budget this year, chiefly because of AI project costs. The main beneficiaries are Huawei, whose Ascend 910C is targeted at 600,000 units in 2026, and Cambricon, the first domestic AI chip firm to turn a profit, targeting 500,000 shipments in 2026.

The market has already tilted sharply. Nvidia's China AI-accelerator share has fallen from about 95% before US export controls to an estimated 8% in 2026, while Huawei's is expected to reach roughly 50%. China's government procurement list has added approved domestic chips from Huawei, Alibaba T-Head, Biren, Hygon, Iluvatar CoreX, MetaX, and Moore Threads. CNBC reported that Goldman Sachs analysts see the pivot accelerating across 2026-28, noting that DeepSeek's latest model runs on eight China-made chips including Huawei and Alibaba T-Head parts. The South China Morning Post cited forecasts that domestic suppliers led by Huawei and Cambricon could hold 56% of China's AI server chip market in 2026, up from 46% in 2025.

Goldman Sachs projects China's top internet firms will invest more than $70bn in data-centre infrastructure in 2026. Morgan Stanley chief China economist Robin Xing expects Beijing to "step up a capex-centric fiscal rollout starting in Q3, primarily focused on AI and power grids rather than consumption."

### Asian PCB and semiconductor stocks tumble after report of >1-year delay to Nvidia's Kyber NVL144 AI server

Research firm SemiAnalysis reported around July 6-7, 2026 that Nvidia's next-generation Kyber NVL144 AI server system faces a delay exceeding one year, citing PCB manufacturing difficulties including a 78-layer backplane, M9-grade materials, and yield and impedance challenges. The report triggered a broad Asia-Pacific selloff across printed-circuit-board and semiconductor names. On July 7, Japan's Ibiden fell 10%, Hong Kong-listed Kingboard Laminates dropped 18%, Korea's Samsung Electro-Mechanics declined 11%, and Taiwan's Elite Material lost 9.95%, Nan Ya PCB 9.28%, Kinsus 8.4%, and Unimicron 5.37%. An MSCI sector gauge fell 8.5% over two weeks.

The declines followed an extraordinary run-up: year-to-date before the selloff, Samsung Electro-Mechanics had risen roughly 600% and Kingboard about 470% on AI-infrastructure expectations, leaving the stocks exposed to any disappointment. An Nvidia spokesperson said, "Our road map is intact." Gary Tan, a portfolio manager at Allspring Global Investments, said, "A delay to Kyber does not necessarily imply weaker AI capital expenditure, but it more likely suggests that Nvidia's most ambitious next-gen system architecture may take longer to deploy." For China, any slippage in Nvidia's leading-edge servers could accelerate enterprise adoption of Huawei's Ascend ecosystem.

### China sentences former official Yang Youlin to death for 2.21 billion yuan corruption

The Changzhou Intermediate People's Court in Jiangsu delivered a death sentence on July 7, 2026 against Yang Youlin, 69, former executive deputy director of the Nanjing Economic and Technological Development Zone Management Committee, for accepting bribes exceeding 2.21 billion yuan ($324-325 million) from 1993 to 2023 in exchange for favours spanning projects, business operations, land grants, and working capital. He was also convicted of embezzlement, misappropriation of public funds, offering bribes, abuse of power, and money laundering, drawing additional sentences ranging from six months to 11.5 years. Public hearings were held in March and April 2026, and the court ordered the seizure of all his personal property.

Yang expressed "guilt and remorse," according to the court. The case sits within Xi Jinping's anti-corruption campaign, under which nearly 5 million officials have been investigated since 2012. Death sentences remain rare in graft cases, typically applied only when bribes exceed 1 billion yuan; the most prominent recent comparison is former Huarong chairman Lai Xiaomin, who was executed in 2021.

### Gansu landslide buries 16, Hubei tornadoes and typhoon rains drive nationwide toll past 15 as Typhoon Bavi looms

A landslide in mountainous Tanchang county, Gansu Province, buried 33 people on July 7, 2026; 17 were rescued and 16 remained buried at reporting time. President Xi Jinping ordered "all-out efforts" to organise emergency rescue, treat the injured, and resettle affected residents, according to CCTV. The same day, "severe convective weather" — thunderstorms, gale-force winds, and tornadoes described as "extremely rare," last seen in May 2021 — struck Hubei Province, killing 11 and injuring 331 across Huangshi, Huanggang, Ezhou, and Xianning. Winds reached 149 kph, damaging 4,800 houses, collapsing 22, and forcing the evacuation of 408 residents in Hubei.

In southern Guangxi, heavy rain from Typhoon Maysak killed at least 4 in Nanning, which raised its flood-control response to the highest Level 1 after a reservoir dam breach; about 48,000 people were evacuated across Guangxi. The combined nationwide toll reached at least 15 dead and more than 300 injured by July 7. Offshore, Super Typhoon Bavi, packing winds near 180 mph (290 kph), struck Guam, Tinian, Saipan, and Rota before tracking toward Taiwan and China's east coast, with landfall expected later in the week.

## 5. China-Nexus Finance & Investment

### USTR opens Section 301 forced-labor tariff hearings; China among 46 economies facing 12.5% additional duty

Beginning at 10:00 a.m. ET on Tuesday, July 7, 2026, the USTR Section 301 Committee convened public hearings at the US International Trade Commission at 500 E Street SW in Washington, DC on proposed responsive tariff actions against 60 economies found to have failed to impose or effectively enforce a prohibition on importing goods made with forced labor. The hearings run through July 9, 2026, following an investigation launched March 12, 2026 under Section 301(b). USTR findings issued June 2, 2026 concluded that 54 of the 60 economies failed to impose or enforce such a prohibition, while 6 imposed but did not effectively enforce it.

The proposed rates split the field: a 10% additional duty on 14 economies — including the EU, Canada, and Mexico — that committed to reciprocal trade agreements, and a 12.5% additional duty on the remaining 46, including China, India, Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, and Hong Kong. Exclusions cover USMCA-qualifying goods, CAFTA-DR duty-free items, certain agricultural products, aerospace and semiconductor goods, and metals in HTSUS chapters 80-81. The written comment deadline was July 6, 2026, and Ambassador Jamieson Greer leads USTR. The action is distinct from the Xinjiang-specific UFLPA import ban already in force against Chinese goods.
