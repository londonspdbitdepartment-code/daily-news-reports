### Japan's 30-Year Bond Auction Draws Solid Demand as Long-End Yields Push Toward 4%

Japan's Ministry of Finance sold ¥900 billion of 30-year government bonds on July 7, 2026, and the bids came in. Issue #91, carrying a 4.0% coupon and maturing in June 2056, drew a bid-to-cover ratio of roughly 4.55 times, with the cut-off yield at the lowest accepted price landing at 3.996%. For an auction that markets had flagged for weeks as a stress test, that was a clean pass.

The tension was in the backdrop. The 30-year yield had already touched around 4.08% in recent sessions — it first cleared 4% on July 2 — levels the Japanese long end had not seen in decades. A run of super-long auctions is scheduled through the summer, and the open question is whether domestic institutions like life insurers will keep absorbing supply as the Bank of Japan retreats from the bond market it dominated for a decade under yield-curve control. The BOJ lifted its short-term policy rate to 0.75% in December 2025, the highest since 1995, and traders now price further hikes ahead.

Today's coverage ratio suggests real demand is still there at 4%. The next super-long sale will test whether it holds.

### Japan Household Spending Rises 1.3% in May in Nominal Terms but Slips 0.4% in Real Terms

Japanese households spent an average of ¥320,345 a month in May 2026, the Statistics Bureau reported July 7 — up 1.3% from a year earlier in nominal terms, but down 0.4% once inflation is stripped out. The real decline is the number that matters for policymakers.

The figure captures the central puzzle of Japan's recovery. Pay is finally rising, yet families are not translating that into more consumption. In the months before May, real household spending had contracted repeatedly even as wages climbed, a divergence that leaves the Bank of Japan cautious about how much domestic demand can absorb higher rates.

The timing sharpened the contrast: hours before the spending data, separate figures showed nominal wages up 3.2% in May. Cash coming in, spending flat — the gap is exactly what the BOJ is watching as it weighs its next move.

### Economy Minister Kiuchi Rebuffs Reports of Low-Rate Pressure as 10-Year Yield Hits 30-Year High

"There is no change to the government's stance that specific monetary policy means fall under the jurisdiction of the BOJ," Economy Minister Minoru Kiuchi told reporters in Tokyo on July 7, 2026, pushing back on reports that the government had leaned on the central bank to keep rates low.

The remark landed as the bond market moved against him. The 10-year JGB yield rose to 2.83%, its highest in roughly 30 years, while the 30-year yield reached 4.08%. Kiuchi's insistence that policy tools belong to the BOJ is a statement about a relationship under strain: with fiscal worries and normalization bets both pushing yields up, any hint that the government wants cheaper money feeds the market's unease rather than calming it.

Investors read the exchange as confirmation that the upward pressure on Japanese yields is structural, not a passing spike. The 10-year yield first cleared 2% only in December 2025.

### Japan's Nominal Wages Rise 3.2% in May for Fourth Straight Month Above 3%, Longest Streak Since 1992

Nominal cash earnings in Japan rose 3.2% year-on-year in May 2026, the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare reported July 7, following a revised 3.6% gain in April. It was the fourth consecutive month of nominal wage growth of at least 3% — the longest such streak since 1992. Base pay, the scheduled portion of earnings that strips out overtime and bonuses, climbed 3.0%.

The more consequential line is real wages: up 1.4% after inflation, a fifth straight month of gains and the longest positive run since 2021. For a central bank that has waited years for wage growth to outpace prices before committing to normalization, this is close to the evidence it wanted. Earnings rose across sectors, from finance and insurance to construction, indicating breadth rather than a one-off in a single industry.

The wage streak sits at the center of the BOJ's case for further hikes. Economists expect the policy rate to reach 1.25% by year-end if the wage-inflation picture holds — though the same-day household data showing real spending still falling is the counterweight.

### Corporate Japan's Rare Earth Warnings Grow Louder as China Keeps Minerals Blocked

Japanese companies are sounding louder alarms over China's rare earth cutoff, the Japan Times reported July 7, as the shortage begins to bleed into the broader economy. The trigger dates to November 2025, when Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi made comments defending Taiwan; since then Beijing has choked off shipments of key minerals to Japan, wielding its dominance of the rare earth market as diplomatic leverage.

Japan is scrambling for alternatives. State-backed JOGMEC has committed $250 million to Australian producer Lynas Rare Earths, and Tokyo is investing in a France-based heavy rare earth refinery targeting 20% of the country's dysprosium and terbium needs — the heavy elements critical to high-performance magnets in electric motors and defense systems. At home, engineers are working to extract rare earths from deep-sea mud near Minamitorishima, the remote Pacific island that marks Japan's easternmost point.

None of these paths is fast. Analysts estimate a full year of Chinese export restrictions would inflict serious damage, and Japan is only four to five months in. The Lynas relationship offers some cushion — a renewed supply deal in March locked in 7,200 metric tons of neodymium and praseodymium annually through 2038 — but heavy rare earths remain the exposed flank across autos, electronics, and defense.

## 2. Politics & Diplomacy

### Japan's Coast Guard Expels Two Chinese Vessels Near Senkakus After They Approach a Fishing Boat

Japan's Coast Guard drove two Chinese Coast Guard ships out of Japanese territorial waters near the Senkaku Islands at 9:20 a.m. Japan time on July 7, 2026. The vessels had approached a Japanese fishing boat operating in the area — the first such approach to a fishing boat since June 10.

The incident fits a pattern that has intensified year after year. China, which claims the uninhabited islands as the Diaoyu Islands, sent government ships into the contiguous zone around them on 357 of 365 days in 2025, a record for the fourth straight year. Incursions into Japan's territorial waters have grown longer and bolder: in May 2026, Chinese ships lingered inside those waters for more than 92 hours, one of the longest intrusions on record. The approach to a fishing vessel is a further escalation, testing how Japan protects civilian boats in contested seas.

Tokyo lodged a protest through diplomatic channels. The friction runs alongside the broader deterioration in ties since Takaichi's Taiwan remarks last November.

### Japan Sends Foreign and Defense Ministers to NATO's Ankara Summit as Takaichi Stays Home

Japan dispatched Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi and Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi to the NATO summit in Ankara, Turkey, on July 7, 2026. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi skipped the trip, citing parliamentary commitments in Tokyo.

The summit's centerpiece is money: NATO members are working toward a target of 5% of GDP on defense, up from the old 2% benchmark, with a 2035 deadline agreed at last year's Hague summit and Washington pressing allies to move faster. Japan attends not as a member but as an Indo-Pacific partner — one of the IP4 grouping alongside South Korea, Australia, and New Zealand, whose ministers met NATO counterparts on the sidelines.

Japan's own commitment is more modest: 2% of GDP by fiscal 2027. But sending both the foreign and defense ministers to Ankara, even with the prime minister absent, marks how far Tokyo has moved toward treating European and Indo-Pacific security as a single theater — a shift unthinkable a decade ago.

### Tokyo Keiki Moves Into Antidrone Systems to Counter UAV Threats

Tokyo Keiki Inc., a Japanese precision instruments maker, is commercializing an antidrone detection and countermeasure system for civilian and military UAV threats, Nikkei Asia reported at 07:15 JST on July 7, 2026.

The move puts a low-profile, roughly 130-year-old company into one of the fastest-growing corners of Japan's defense buildup. Small drones fly below the coverage of conventional radar, which is exactly the gap Tokyo Keiki is targeting. The company brings decades of experience supplying equipment for military aircraft and naval vessels, and it has already tied up with startup Metro Weather to mass-produce Doppler LiDAR sensors capable of spotting objects too small for radar to catch.

The timing tracks a broader Japanese push. As drones reshape modern conflict and manpower shortfalls press the Self-Defense Forces, Tokyo has moved to institutionalize counter-drone capability across its forces — and legacy industrial firms are repositioning to supply it.

## 4. Society & Livelihood

### Japan's Rice Price Outlook Sinks to Record-Low 19, Ninth Straight Month Below 50

The diffusion index for Japan's rice price outlook over the next three months fell 4 points in June to 19, matching the lowest level on record, previously set in August 2014, according to a survey by a group that includes the National Federation of Agricultural Cooperative Associations, or Zen-noh. It was the ninth consecutive month with the index below 50, the line separating expectations of rising and falling prices.

The reading marks the unwinding of the crisis that gripped Japanese consumers through 2024 and 2025, when rice shortages sent prices soaring and became a political flashpoint. Now the pressure runs the other way: a record 58% of surveyed rice-industry members cited high inventory levels, and the supply-demand outlook index edged up 1 point to 20 as bigger harvests and rebuilt private stockpiles point to ample supply.

For households, relief is real but partial. Analysts expect retail prices to settle in the ¥3,000 to ¥3,800 per five-kilogram range through 2026 and 2027 — well below the crisis peak, but still above the roughly ¥2,000 that shoppers paid in early 2024.

### Keisei Electric Plans New Limited Express Directly Linking Haneda and Narita Airports by the 2030s

Keisei Electric Railway announced plans on July 7, 2026 for a new limited express connecting Tokyo's two main airports, Haneda and Narita, targeted to begin in the 2030s. An intermediate Narita-Oshiage service is planned for fiscal year 2028, with the full route running through-connections via the Asakusa subway line and the Keikyu line.

For decades, moving between Haneda and Narita has meant a slow, transfer-heavy slog across metropolitan Tokyo — an awkward gap for a country chasing record inbound tourism. Keisei, which already runs the Skyliner express to Narita, is positioning the link as travel infrastructure for a visitor boom: Japan drew a record 41-plus million foreign visitors in 2025 and is targeting 60 million by 2030.

The 2030s target leaves ample room for delay, but the fiscal 2028 interim service gives the plan a near-term milestone to measure against.

### Super Typhoon Bavi Tracks Toward Southern Japan With Okinawa's Sakishima Islands in Its Path

Super Typhoon Bavi sat about 1,800 kilometers southeast of Okinawa as of Tuesday morning, July 7, 2026, packing maximum sustained winds of 250 km/h — a strong Category 4 storm. The Japan Meteorological Agency warned of potential impacts on Okinawa's Sakishima Islands from Friday into Saturday, with strong winds, heavy rain, and high waves.

The Sakishima Islands — Ishigaki, Miyako, and Iriomote among them — are remote, tourist-heavy outposts far to Japan's southwest, where a direct hit strains limited infrastructure and can strand visitors for days. Bavi is the third Category 5 typhoon of 2026, having briefly reached that peak intensity before easing; forecasters expect its track to carry it northeast of Taiwan before a landfall in eastern China.

Whether the storm brushes Okinawa or passes to its south hinges on a track still days from resolving. The JMA's Friday-to-Saturday window gives the Sakishima Islands a narrow runway to prepare.

### York Holdings Opens IPO Office Targeting Fiscal 2028 Listing and Eyes Eastern Japan Supermarket Deals

York Holdings set up an IPO office in July 2026 and is building toward a Tokyo Stock Exchange listing in the fiscal year ending February 2028, the Japan Times reported July 7. The supermarket and retail group is the business Bain Capital carved out of Seven & i Holdings last year for roughly $5.37 billion, in one of Japan's largest private-equity buyouts.

Bain owns about 60% of York, with Seven & i and the founding Ito family holding the rest. The portfolio spans 29 subsidiaries, anchored by the Ito-Yokado grocery chain and including baby-goods retailer Akachan Honpo and Japan's Denny's restaurant franchise. Management is in active talks with supermarket operators across eastern Japan — the Kanto and Tohoku regions — aiming to thicken its store network and fill geographic gaps, with executives saying acquisition opportunities are likely to surface over the next two and a half years.

A fiscal 2028 listing would mark Bain's exit path from the deal — turning a take-private supermarket carve-out into a public company, with bolt-on M&A meant to bulk up the business before it faces the market.

### JAXA Releases Hayabusa2's Close-Up Photos of Asteroid Torifune From Its July 6 Flyby

JAXA released close-range photographs of asteroid Torifune on July 7, 2026, taken by the Hayabusa2 spacecraft one second before its closest approach the previous evening. On July 6 at 18:30 JST, the probe swept within a few hundred meters of the asteroid at speeds exceeding 18,000 km/h.

Torifune is a "snowman-like" contact-binary — two lobes fused together — roughly 450 meters long and sitting about 100 million kilometers from Earth. Capturing a sharp image at that closing speed is a feat of timing, and the mission gathered data from its NIRS3, TIR, and LIDAR instruments during the pass, the kind of characterization that feeds planetary-defense research on how to deflect a hazardous body.

"I was in awe about the fact that Hayabusa2 took such a good picture in a fleeting moment of the flyby," said Yuya Mimasu, head of JAXA's Hayabusa2 extended mission team. The flyby is part of the probe's extended tour after it delivered asteroid Ryugu samples to Earth in 2020.
