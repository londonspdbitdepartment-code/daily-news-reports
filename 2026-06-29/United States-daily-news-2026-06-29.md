### Alphabet displaces Verizon in the Dow as Iran ceasefire lifts US stock futures

Alphabet Inc. Class A shares (GOOGL) replaced Verizon Communications in the 30-stock Dow Jones Industrial Average before the opening bell on Monday, June 29, 2026 — the 54th adjustment in the index's 130-plus-year history. The swap, which S&P Dow Jones Indices announced June 23, hands the price-weighted benchmark one of its heaviest hitters: at $345.29 a share, Alphabet enters as the sixth most influential stock in the Dow, with a market cap of $4.1 trillion and a cloud division that grew sales 63% in Q1 2026.

The mechanics of a price-weighted index explain why the change matters. The Dow ranks members by share price, not company size, so a $4 trillion firm trading near $345 carries far more sway than a telecom giant whose stock sits in the mid-$40s. Verizon, a Dow component for 22 years since April 2004, was dropped precisely because its $45.68 share price had shrunk to just 0.5% of the index's weighting; its shares gained 36% since 2004 excluding dividends, a thin return over two decades. With Alphabet in, five of the "Magnificent Seven" are now Dow members, alongside Nvidia, Amazon, Apple, and Microsoft. Nike, trading below $42, faces potential next removal.

The reshuffle landed on a buoyant tape. With the US and Iran halting strikes over the weekend, S&P 500 futures rose 0.86% to 7,465.25, Nasdaq-100 futures climbed 1.18% to 29,715.75, and Dow futures added 233 points (0.45%) to 52,442; the VIX eased 0.76% to 18.27. That followed a brutal prior week in which the S&P 500 and Nasdaq fell more than 2% and 4.5%. Commodities split: Brent rose 0.6% to $73 and WTI gained 0.9% to near $70, while gold fell 1.06% to $4,053 an ounce. Markets close Friday for Independence Day.

### Johnson to send 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act to Trump, starting the 10-day clock

House Speaker Mike Johnson said he would transmit the enrolled 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act to President Trump on Monday, June 29, 2026, starting the constitutionally mandated 10-day presidential review clock that gives Trump until about July 10 to sign or veto. The bipartisan measure — the largest housing affordability legislation in decades — passed the Senate 85-5 on June 22 and the House 358-32 on June 23. The final text stitched together provisions from more than 60 bills introduced across both chambers, 36 of them with bipartisan sponsors.

The substance reaches deep into the housing supply chain. The bill streamlines environmental reviews under the National Environmental Policy Act, expands categorical exclusions for housing projects, and lets HUD delegate reviews to states and localities — all aimed at the regulatory delays that inflate construction costs. It restricts large institutional investors that own at least 350 single-family homes from buying more, with carve-outs for build-to-rent, and creates competitive grants that steer federal money toward localities that build more housing. It also temporarily bars the Federal Reserve from establishing a digital dollar.

The hold-up is political, not substantive. Trump abruptly canceled a planned June 23 signing ceremony, posting: "Today's Housing News Conference and Signing is hereby canceled until such time as we pass the desperately needed SAVE AMERICA ACT, which I consider to be a National Emergency." The SAVE America Act — requiring photo ID and proof of citizenship to vote — is blocked by Senate Democrats and lacks 60 votes, turning the housing law into leverage. Johnson said he and Trump were "exactly on the same page" and expected a signature within the window. If Trump neither signs nor vetoes within 10 days while Congress is in session, the bill becomes law; Congress plans pro forma sessions to block a pocket veto. NAHB CEO Jim Tobin said, "I do believe that this legislation will become law at some point."

### Trump's reactor pilot reaches two of three reactors critical before July 4, drawing safety warnings

Five days before July 4, two US companies have achieved criticality under the DOE Reactor Pilot Program: Antares Nuclear on June 4 and Valar Atomics on June 18, the latter producing tens of kilowatts of heat from a reactor core housed in a tent-like structure in the Utah desert. A third, Radiant, was assembling its reactor at Idaho National Laboratory's DOME facility and expected to begin testing by July 4 but was unlikely to reach full criticality by the deadline; Aalo Atomics operates a Critical Test Reactor facility in Idaho Falls.

The target traces to Trump's May 2025 executive order, which directed the DOE to facilitate at least three operational experimental reactors by July 4, 2026, the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence. To hit it, the program cut regulatory requirements — the DOE rewrote safety and security standards and exempted new reactors from environmental reviews, consulting the companies involved but not the public. The designs themselves lean on newer fuel: Radiant's reactor uses uranium-filled fuel spheres, nicknamed "nuclear gobstoppers," that allow higher operating temperatures and resist meltdown.

Safety experts question the pace. Edwin Lyman, director of nuclear power safety at the Union of Concerned Scientists, called the program "essentially an exercise in public relations" and said the deregulation "takes us back to the 1950s." The push extends beyond the demonstrators: the administration separately announced $17.5 billion in loans for 10 new large conventional reactors on June 23, 2026.

### Omen AI raises $31 million Series A to watch data-center coolant in real time

Omen AI, a US data center technology startup founded in 2024, announced a $31 million Series A led by Nava Ventures on June 29, 2026, bringing total funding to $40 million. The company's miniaturized spectrometer monitors the health of liquid coolant inside AI data centers in real time, detecting bacterial contamination, pump wear, seal degradation, and metal particulate levels before they cause a failure.

The problem is a byproduct of the AI build-out. As accelerators such as Nvidia GPUs throw off extreme heat, operators have shifted to liquid cooling with higher water content, which raises the risk of bacterial growth inside the loop. A contamination event can force a five-to-six-hour emergency flush, costing millions in downtime. CEO Zach Laberge framed the pitch around visibility: "You're not risking huge amounts of downtime because you have no insight" into fluid chemistry. Omen AI works with about 12 data center clients, including GPU cloud operator TensorWave, and competitor Pyxis launched a rival product the same month.

The market backdrop is enormous. Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, and Amazon are projected to spend roughly $700 billion on AI infrastructure in 2026, most of it on data centers — the spending wave that makes a niche monitoring tool a potentially large business.

### Dangerous heat wave engulfs half the US ahead of July 4

The National Weather Service warned on June 29, 2026 of the first widespread heat wave of summer, reaching more than half the continental US. Forecasters expected "more than 100 record-high temperatures through Saturday night" and "more than 250 record-warm overnight lows" — a dual signature of climate-driven heat, where the failure of temperatures to drop at night compounds the daytime danger.

Heat indices are projected at 100 to 110°F (40 to 43°C) and as high as 115°F (46°C) in the worst-hit areas. Tens of millions face Level 3 of 4 "major" or Level 4 of 4 "extreme" heat risk from the Deep South up through Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan, New York, and the Mid-Atlantic. Cities under alerts include New York, Washington D.C., Philadelphia, Baltimore, Chicago, St. Louis, Detroit, Dallas, Little Rock, and Memphis, with Phoenix, Las Vegas, and central Texas also affected. In many cities overnight lows stay in the 70s°F, denying bodies the recovery window that prevents heat illness from accumulating.

The CDC lists the warning signs to watch — muscle cramping, dizziness, heavy sweating, shortness of breath, headaches, weakness, and nausea — and residents are advised to "limit outdoor activity, stay hydrated and ensure access to air-conditioning and other cooling areas." Markets close Friday for Independence Day.

### About 20 US airports use private screeners instead of TSA; $803 million San Francisco contract affirmed

About 20 US airports operate under the federal Screening Partnership Program, using private contractors rather than TSA officers to screen passengers and baggage. The largest are Kansas City International and San Francisco International, with others including Sarasota, Florida; Atlantic City, New Jersey; Tupelo, Mississippi; and Yellowstone, Montana. At Kansas City, VMD Corp. of McLean, Virginia runs operations, with its officers performing all screening while TSA keeps an audit and oversight role. VMD program manager Gabe Murphy summed up the arrangement: "Their SOP is our SOP."

The model dates to the aftermath of the September 11, 2001 attacks, when Congress created the TSA and allowed airports to opt for certified private contractors instead. Federal law requires those contractors to pay employees at rates equivalent to TSA workers — a parity that grew more expensive after TSA officers won pay increases through a 2023 omnibus bill and an AFGE collective bargaining agreement in 2024.

The program is expanding at its biggest sites. The GAO recently affirmed VMD's $803 million proposal to take over screening at San Francisco International, and Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International — among the world's busiest airports — is among those weighing a shift to private security.

### Comcast to spin off NBCUniversal and Sky tax-free; shares jump 21%

Comcast Corporation announced on June 29, 2026 that it will split into two separately traded public companies through a tax-free spinoff of its media and entertainment assets. The new entity, NBCUniversal, will hold Universal's film and TV studios, the NBC and Telemundo networks, Peacock, Bravo, Universal theme parks, and Sky, the British broadcaster Comcast acquired in 2018. Analyst Adam Crisafulli put the investor mood plainly: "Comcast shares have traded poorly due in large part to concerns about the secular outlook for the broadband and cable businesses." Leadership divides with the assets. Co-CEO Mike Cavanagh becomes CEO of the new NBCUniversal; former CFO Michael Angelakis becomes CEO of the remaining Comcast and a strategic adviser during the transition; chairman and co-CEO Brian Roberts stays involved with both. Comcast will retain up to 19.9% of the new entity for one year, with the close expected within about 12 months pending board and regulatory approvals. The move follows Comcast's November 2024 spinoff of USA, Oxygen, E!, SYFY, Golf Channel, CNBC, and MSNBC. Separately, Charter Communications gained about 20% on reports of SpaceX talks.

### Rocket Lab to acquire Iridium in $8 billion space communications deal

Rocket Lab Corporation (Nasdaq: RKLB) and Iridium Communications Inc. (Nasdaq: IRDM) announced on June 29, 2026 a definitive agreement under which Rocket Lab will acquire Iridium in a cash-and-stock transaction valued at about $8.0 billion enterprise value. Iridium shareholders receive $27.00 in cash plus Rocket Lab shares worth $54.00 combined per share — a 24.1% premium to Iridium's last close — subject to a collar between $67.50 and $112.50 per Rocket Lab share. To fund the cash portion, Rocket Lab secured a $3.6 billion, 364-day senior secured bridge facility from Deutsche Bank and Wells Fargo.

The logic is vertical integration. The deal pairs Rocket Lab's launch vehicles and satellite manufacturing with Iridium's global L-band network, licensed spectrum, and more than 2.55 million active subscribers across government, defense, aviation, maritime, and commercial markets — and owning the network is expected to eliminate third-party launch costs for future constellation deployments and replenishment. Founder and CEO Sir Peter Beck said, "This is a defining moment for the space industry... we have the capability to unlock entirely new markets." Iridium CEO Matt Desch framed the rationale around speed: "Success will come from those who can bring new innovations to space quickly and sustain them over time as efficiently as possible."

Both boards approved the deal unanimously, and Iridium directors committed to vote in favor. It is expected to close in mid-2027, subject to Iridium stockholder approval and regulatory clearances. Rocket Lab shares rose about 9 to 12% in premarket trading, and Iridium about 20 to 22%.

### Scheffler and Hovland tied at 21-under force Monday playoff at Travelers Championship

The final round of the 2026 Travelers Championship, a PGA Tour Signature Event at TPC River Highlands in Cromwell, Connecticut, ended on Sunday, June 29, 2026 with world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler of the USA and Norway's Viktor Hovland tied at 21-under 259 after 72 holes, forcing a Monday playoff. Scheffler carries a 2-2 PGA Tour playoff record into it; for Hovland it is his first. Scheffler said, "It's more fun when you're making the ones to win. But to keep yourself in it is also nice." ### Venezuela earthquake death toll reaches 1,450 as US military aids rescue; survivor freed after 106 hours

As of June 29, 2026, the death toll from Venezuela's twin earthquakes — a magnitude 7.2 followed seconds later by a 7.5, which struck the country's Caribbean coast on June 24–25 — reached at least 1,450, with approximately 3,150 injured and more than 46,000 listed as missing. International rescue teams from 27 countries, totaling 2,244 specialists and 140 search dogs, kept searching collapsed buildings in Caracas, La Guaira, and surrounding areas.

The grim arithmetic of survival still produced occasional breakthroughs. On June 29, Aaron Levi Cantillo was pulled alive from the rubble after 106 hours trapped, following a 43-hour extraction; Acting President Delcy Rodriguez shared video of the rescue. Earlier survivors included Belkys Josefina Barreto García, 60, freed after 86 hours by a Salvadoran crew, and an 11-year-old boy rescued by a Colombian team. US military assets joined the response: 100 US Air Force airmen arrived to repair Simón Bolívar International Airport in Maiquetía, 130 US Marines deployed to La Guaira port to facilitate humanitarian aid, and helicopters from the USS Lauderdale conducted aerial surveys.

Recovery in La Guaira stood at 75% electricity, 68% potable water, and 90% of the road network restored, per Rodriguez. The UN Development Programme estimated economic damage at $6.7 billion, about 6% of Venezuela's GDP, with 8.6 million people exposed to moderate-to-severe shaking.
