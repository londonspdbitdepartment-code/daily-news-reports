### Commerce Secretary's June 30 copper report sets up Trump decision on 15-30% refined-metal tariffs

A statutory deadline lands on the President's desk June 30, 2026: the Secretary of Commerce must deliver an update on domestic copper markets — refining capacity and the market for refined copper — so Trump can decide whether to impose a phased universal import duty on refined copper of 15 percent from January 1, 2027, rising to 30 percent from January 1, 2028. The June 2026 White House proclamation "Further Adjusting the Tariff Regimes for Imports of Aluminum, Steel, and Copper" mandated the review, picking up a recommendation from the June 30, 2025 Section 232 report.

Refined copper has so far escaped the levies. When the administration acted in August 2025, it imposed a 50 percent tariff on semi-finished copper products — pipes, wires, rods, sheets, tubes — and copper-intensive derivatives, but spared copper input materials and refined metal, which accounts for roughly half of all US copper imports. The June 30 update is the trigger that decides whether that exemption ends. The 2025 report went further still, recommending a domestic sales requirement for copper input materials starting at 25% in 2027, a 25% domestic sales requirement for high-quality copper scrap, and export controls on that scrap.

A 15-30% wall around refined copper would reach US manufacturing, electronics, the defense industrial base, and the country's largest copper suppliers. Once the Secretary's update is in hand, the President decides whether to proceed.

### Supreme Court set to rule on Trump's bid to end birthright citizenship

The Supreme Court is expected to rule June 30, 2026 — the final day of its term — on President Trump's executive order to end birthright citizenship, a guarantee the 14th Amendment has carried for more than a century. The case, Trump v. Barbara (No. 25-365), tests whether the President can unilaterally strip citizenship from children born on US soil to undocumented parents or those on temporary visas. NPR reported the opinion was expected at approximately 10:00 AM ET, the term's first and likely only opinion day of the final week.

During April 1 oral arguments, which Trump attended in person, a majority appeared skeptical of the administration's theory. Chief Justice Roberts and four other justices questioned whether the order squared with longstanding constitutional interpretation. The plaintiffs leaned on United States v. Wong Kim Ark, the 1898 precedent holding the Citizenship Clause covers nearly every child born in the country; the Solicitor General argued the clause was meant for the formerly enslaved and their descendants, not the children of temporary visitors or people here unlawfully.

The order already reached the Court once. After the justices limited universal injunctions in Trump v. CASA last year, a New Hampshire federal judge blocked enforcement against a class of affected newborns. If the Court rules against Trump, the order stays enjoined. If it rules for him, it would be the most significant change to birthright citizenship since the 14th Amendment was ratified in 1868.

A ransomware group calling itself "World Leaks" hit Tata Electronics, one of Apple's primary iPhone assembly contractors in India, leaking more than 200,000 files totaling approximately 630 GB, CNBC reported June 30, 2026. The breach strikes at the center of Apple's "China Plus One" manufacturing push. That a criminal ransomware crew — not a state actor — pulled this off raises questions about how tightly Apple audits the security of its tiered suppliers.

### America's first comprehensive state AI law dies on its effective date, gutted under federal pressure

June 30, 2026 was supposed to be the day Colorado SB 24-205 — the first comprehensive US state AI regulation — took effect, imposing a duty of care on AI developers and deployers to prevent algorithmic discrimination, requiring algorithmic impact assessments, and granting a rebuttable presumption of compliance to firms following the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. It never went live. Governor Jared Polis signed SB 26-189 on May 14, 2026, repealing and replacing the law with a lighter framework built around transparency and disclosure, stripping out the duty of care, the impact assessments and the compliance presumption. The replacement takes effect January 1, 2027.

The retreat came under direct federal pressure and on a compressed clock. On April 9, 2026, xAI sued to challenge SB 24-205's constitutionality; on April 24, the US Department of Justice moved to intervene in support — the first time the federal government has sought to strike down a state AI law. That intervention flowed from Trump's executive order, which had criticized SB 24-205 for potentially forcing AI models to produce false results, and which stood up a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI statutes on preemption and Commerce Clause grounds.

The collapse hands a win to the federal preemption push and the "innovation-first" camp, and widens the gap between the US approach and the EU AI Act's prescriptive model — a divergence that will shape how other states legislate through 2026 and 2027.

### US scientists pack up for the UK as Trump's research cuts trigger a brain drain

Prominent US scientists are relocating to the United Kingdom after deep cuts to federal research funding under the Trump administration, NPR reported June 30, 2026. The story profiles Megan Peters, a cognitive neuroscientist who left UC Irvine for University College London, and Ron Mangun, a neuroscientist who left UC Davis for the University of Birmingham — both citing the elimination of their NIH and NSF grants as the trigger. The UK government has stood up a targeted recruitment effort, the "Science Superpower Programme," dangling enhanced salaries and research infrastructure, and UK universities report 3-5x the normal volume of applications from US-based researchers for 2026 faculty posts versus 2025.

The pull is matched by a hard push at home. Federal agencies began terminating already-funded grants at NIH in February and later at NSF, with thousands of awards cancelled or suspended. The European Research Council has seen US early-career grant applications nearly triple, from 60 for its 2024 call to 169 for 2026, and in one survey of more than 1,600 US scientists, three-quarters said they had thought about leaving the country.

Unlike layoffs at a federal agency, the loss of leading researchers to foreign institutions is hard to reverse — a structural erosion of US scientific capacity that compounds year over year, with the UK, Germany and Canada all actively recruiting.

### Parent PLUS borrowers face a June 30 consolidation deadline to keep a path to income-driven repayment

Parent PLUS loan borrowers face a June 30, 2026 deadline to fold their loans into a Direct Consolidation Loan if they want access to income-driven repayment, NPR reported. Parent PLUS loans cannot enroll in IDR plans directly, but borrowers who consolidate before the deadline can enroll in the Income-Contingent Repayment (ICR) plan. An estimated 3.5 million Parent PLUS borrowers could be affected.

The deadline arrives amid legal turbulence over student-loan policy — ongoing battles over the Biden-era forgiveness programs and the Trump administration's rollback of IDR — leaving the underlying framework in flux even as the clock runs out for families.

### Comcast to spin off NBCUniversal and Sky, ending a 15-year media-and-broadband marriage

Comcast Corporation announced June 29, 2026 it will split into two independent public companies via a tax-free spinoff of NBCUniversal and Sky. The new media company will hold NBC, Telemundo, MSNBC, CNBC, Bravo, Peacock streaming (65 million subscribers), Universal film studios and British broadcaster Sky; Comcast keeps Xfinity broadband (about 32 million subscribers), Xfinity Wireless and Comcast Business. Comcast will retain a 19.9% stake in the new company, and current president Mike Cavanagh becomes CEO of the new NBCUniversal entity. The split is expected to close in roughly 12 months. Comcast shares jumped 21% ($4.85 to $28.02) in premarket trading after the June 29 8-K filing with the SEC.

This is the larger sequel to a 2024 move. In November 2024 Comcast hived off a portfolio of linear cable networks — including CNBC and MSNBC, since rebranded — into a separate company, Versant, which went public in January around $45 a share. The NBCUniversal spinoff now severs the deeper seam: a regulated broadband infrastructure business on one side, a competitive content and entertainment business on the other, unwinding the bet that the two created more value together than apart. The 21% pop signals the market had been pricing in separation as value-unlocking, and the deal sets up antitrust scrutiny over Peacock's relationship with Comcast's cable distribution.

The Supreme Court handed President Trump sweeping new authority over the federal government in its 2025-26 term but drew hard limits in the two areas most sensitive to markets: the Federal Reserve and tariff power, according to an Axios analysis published June 30, 2026. The rulings give the President firmer command of the bureaucracy — including the power to fire independent agency heads in Trump v. Slaughter — signaling that regulatory policy will swing harder with each election. Yet the Court preserved Fed independence in Trump v. Cook, 5-4, and blocked an emergency tariff expansion, fencing off the levers whose politicization investors feared most.

The contradiction is the story: Trump won broad executive power, but the Court specifically carved out the Fed's grip on interest rates and the legal ceiling on emergency tariffs — the two channels through which presidential control would most directly rattle markets. That split signals accelerating deregulation of the administrative state alongside preserved monetary-policy stability, a framing that feeds directly into investor positioning for the second half of 2026.

### Cottonwood Fire hits 94,000 acres as three federal firefighters die on the Utah-Colorado line

The Cottonwood Fire in Utah's Fishlake National Forest grew to approximately 93,918 acres by June 30, 2026 at just 4% containment, making it the largest active wildfire in the United States. The blaze started June 22 and has destroyed roughly 150 structures. On June 27-28, three federal wildland firefighters were killed in a burnover incident on the Colorado-Utah border while responding to the Snyder Fire, which had grown to 28,000 acres at 0% containment in Mesa County, Colorado. The dead were identified as Emily Barker, 38, Nick Hutcherson, 27, and Sydney Watson, 27; two others were injured.

Utah wildfires have burned over 180,000 acres in 2026, the busiest fire year since 2020 and already past 2025's full-year total in half the time. The setup was primed: the 2025-26 winter was Utah's warmest on record with one of its lowest snowpacks, and crews battled single-digit humidity, wind gusts near 45 mph and fuel moisture as low as 2%. Hundreds of residents in Marysvale, Junction and Circleville were placed under evacuation orders. The National Weather Service issued a rare "Particularly Dangerous Situation" Red Flag Warning — the first ever from its Salt Lake City office.

### How the 2025-26 Supreme Court term reshaped executive power, privacy and voting rights

The Supreme Court's 2025-26 term, concluding June 30, 2026, will rank among the most consequential in modern history for the pace and scope of constitutional change, Newsweek's end-of-term synthesis argues. Its rundown: Trump v. Slaughter (6-3) overturned Humphrey's Executor and gave the President power to fire independent agency heads; Trump v. Cook (5-4) blocked Trump from immediately firing Fed Governor Lisa Cook, preserving some Fed independence; Watson v. RNC (5-4) upheld state laws counting mail-in ballots arriving after Election Day, rebuffing Trump's attacks on mail voting; and Chatrie v. United States (6-3) restricted geofence warrants as Fourth Amendment searches.

The pattern is two-sided: the Court expanded presidential power while drawing specific limits, with the Fed and tariff cases the clearest constraints. Structural cases generally split 6-3 along the conservative majority, but Fed independence and digital privacy went 5-4, with Justice Kavanaugh crossing ideological lines. The term's arc — broad executive empowerment, fenced off precisely where it would most destabilize markets and elections — is the constitutional record the next decade of administrative law will be argued against.
