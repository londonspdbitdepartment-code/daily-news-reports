### EU member states give US trade deal final approval ahead of Trump car-tariff deadline

EU member states voted June 25, 2026 to give final approval to the bloc's trade agreement with the United States, closing roughly a year of halting ratification that at points threatened to unravel the whole arrangement. The deal erases EU levies on US industrial goods and some agricultural products; in return, the US applies a 15% tariff ceiling on EU exports.

The vote landed just ahead of a deadline set by President Trump, who had threatened to impose 25% tariffs on EU cars from July 4, 2026 if the agreement was not locked in. That threat was the latest turn in pressure tactics Washington had applied since 2025.

The framework traces back to a July 27, 2025 political handshake between Trump and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen at his Turnberry golf resort in Scotland, which set a 15% rate across most EU exports — cars, auto parts, pharmaceuticals and semiconductors — down from the 30% Trump had earlier floated. Ratification then dragged: the European Parliament cleared the deal on June 16 by 440 votes to 151, with 50 abstentions, before member states delivered the final sign-off. A safeguard lets Parliament request suspension of the tariff concessions if the US keeps duties above 15% on European steel and aluminium past the end of 2026.

### EU residential construction prices up 48.2% over the past decade, Eurostat reports

Residential construction prices across the EU rose 48.2% between 2015 and 2025, Eurostat reported June 25, 2026, with the burden falling unevenly across the bloc. The steepest increases hit central and eastern member states: Bulgaria led at +166.1%, followed by Hungary (+155.4%), Romania (+130.7%), Lithuania (+125.3%) and Estonia (+118.5%).

At the other end, prices barely moved in southern Europe and parts of the west. Italy posted the smallest rise at +17.0%, just ahead of Greece (+17.3%), with Finland (+22.1%), France (+28.8%) and Germany (+31.0%) also well below the EU average.

The decade-long climb was front-loaded. Annual growth peaked at +12.2% in 2022 — the year labour, material and energy costs surged across Europe amid supply-chain disruption and soaring commodity prices — before cooling sharply to +6.9% in 2023, +2.3% in 2024 and +1.3% in 2025.

### Ukraine Recovery Conference opens in Gdańsk without Zelensky

The Ukraine Recovery Conference (URC 2026) opened June 25, 2026 in Gdańsk, Poland, with Prime Minister Yuliia Svyrydenko leading Kyiv's delegation in place of President Volodymyr Zelensky, who stayed away after a diplomatic row with incoming Polish President Karol Nawrocki. Commission President von der Leyen, Polish PM Donald Tusk and European Council President António Costa delivered opening remarks. The European Commission disbursed a €3.2 billion tranche from the Ukraine Support Loan mechanism to coincide with the event, while over 80 Ukrainian companies took part in a business forum.

The conference is the annual marquee event for marshalling reconstruction money for Ukraine. Held outside the country since Russia's 2022 full-scale invasion, it has previously convened in London, Berlin and Rome.

### EU Environment Council debates softening the 2035 combustion-engine phase-out

EU environment ministers convened in Luxembourg on June 25, 2026 under the Cyprus Presidency for an orientation debate on the Commission's December 2025 proposal to revise CO2 standards for cars and vans. The plan would lower the 2035 zero-emission target from 100% to 90%, allowing the remaining 10% to be offset through sustainable fuels and low-carbon steel.

The revision matters because the original 100% target effectively banned new combustion-engine cars from 2035. Under the proposal, the 10% offset — split between alternative fuels and EU-made low-carbon steel — would keep the door open for plug-in hybrids, range extenders and combustion engines running on e-fuels or biofuels beyond that date, alongside fully electric and hydrogen vehicles. Parliament and Council are working out their positions through the first half of 2026.

Ministers also held a policy debate on the European Water Resilience Strategy, which sets a 10% water-consumption reduction target by 2030 and addresses PFAS contamination, and looked ahead to COP31 in Antalya in November 2026. Reform of the REACH chemicals regulation was also on the agenda.

### EU restricts visas for Somali nationals over weak readmission cooperation

The Council of the EU adopted a decision June 25, 2026 tightening visa rules for Somali nationals, citing insufficient cooperation from Mogadishu on taking back citizens staying irregularly in the bloc. Member states will no longer issue multiple-entry visas to Somali applicants, fee waivers for diplomatic and service passport holders are removed, and standard processing time is extended from 15 to 45 calendar days.

The measure runs on Article 25a of the Visa Code, a leverage mechanism that lets the EU dial visa terms up or down according to how well a third country cooperates on returns. Member states had reported chronic problems — difficulty identifying Somali nationals, delays in issuing emergency travel documents, and slow or absent responses to readmission requests. The restrictions are temporary and can be lifted if cooperation improves.

### EU Commission moves to designate AWS and Azure as DMA cloud gatekeepers

The European Commission informed Amazon and Microsoft on June 25, 2026 of its preliminary view that their cloud services, AWS and Azure, should be designated gatekeepers under the Digital Markets Act — AWS as the largest cloud provider in the EU and Azure the second-largest. The move follows a seven-month investigation and is notable because neither service meets the DMA's quantitative thresholds for automatic designation.

Those thresholds — annual EU turnover of at least €7.5 billion, market capitalisation of at least €75 billion, and 45 million monthly EU users — are normally the trigger. Here the Commission relied instead on a qualitative assessment, arguing the two platforms act as an essential gateway between businesses and their EU customers. A gatekeeper designation would impose obligations limiting self-preferencing and mandating interoperability and data portability. A final decision is expected later in 2026, with compliance deadlines following.

Both companies pushed back. Amazon said the position "disregards the breadth of cloud services available in the market." Microsoft said it was "concerned that Google Cloud and Gemini are being ignored."

### EU breaks ground on permanent GOVSATCOM Hub in Cologne

The European Commission held a groundbreaking ceremony June 25, 2026 for the permanent EU GOVSATCOM Hub site in Cologne, Germany, the program that pools secure satellite communications for EU institutions and member states. Operational since January 2026, GOVSATCOM runs from two permanent hubs, in Cologne and Athens.

Attendees included Catherine Kavvada, the Commission's director for secured and connected space; EUSPA executive director Rodrigo da Costa; North Rhine-Westphalia Minister-President Hendrik Wüst; German federal government parliamentary state secretary Matthias Hauer; and DLR deputy chair Stéphane Beemelmans.

GOVSATCOM is the bridge to IRIS², the EU's planned multi-orbit broadband constellation pitched as Europe's answer to Starlink. The €10.6 billion IRIS² system, built around 264 low-Earth-orbit and 18 medium-Earth-orbit satellites, is due to deploy by 2030 to give the bloc sovereign, secure connectivity independent of commercial mega-constellations.

Meteorologists have pointed to climate change as a clear driver of both the intensity and the unusually early onset.

### Parisians bake under the city's historic zinc rooftops

Attic residents living directly beneath Paris's zinc roofs are facing dangerous heat as the city swelters, a Euronews investigation published June 25, 2026 found. A 2003 French heatwave study — that year's heat was blamed for some 15,000 deaths — found attic dwellers carried four times the mortality risk, and a 2023 Lancet study ranked Paris highest for heat-related death risk among 30 European capitals.

About 75% of Paris rooftops are clad in zinc, the metal sheeting that gives the city its celebrated grey skyline and is recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage. The same metal absorbs and conducts heat, turning the apartments beneath into ovens, while heritage rules restrict insulation and exterior shutters that might cool them.

Residents Amelie Kenney and Francesca Pilia, 23, who pay €735 a month, described daytime temperatures inside their apartment topping 40°C and nights stuck around 25°C. Those under the roofs are often there because they cannot afford larger, lower-floor flats less exposed to the sun.

### LIFE WetLands4CLIMATE wins top Climate Action prize at EU LIFE Awards 2026

The LIFE WetLands4CLIMATE project won the Climate Action category at the LIFE Awards 2026, the European Commission's CINEA agency confirmed at a June 25, 2026 ceremony. The four-year, €2.1 million project ran across three Spanish autonomous communities — Castilla y León, Castilla-La Mancha and Comunitat Valenciana — demonstrating that managing and restoring Mediterranean wetlands can mitigate climate change while protecting biodiversity.

The project recorded 3,690 tonnes of CO2-equivalent savings and restored 58 hectares of coastal lagoons, 75 hectares of vegetation and 213 hectares of salt meadows. Its sediment-management approach delivered a 25% to 45% improvement in climate mitigation, and more than 50 companies took part in knowledge-transfer workshops.

The 2026 edition marked the 20th anniversary of the awards. Alongside WetLands4CLIMATE, LIFE Danube floodplains took the Nature Protection and Biodiversity prize and LIFE Turn to e-circular won the Circular Economy and Quality of Life category.

### UK House of Commons holds Windrush Day 2026 debate

The UK House of Commons held a general debate on Windrush Day 2026 on June 25, 2026, scheduled by the Backbench Business Committee following an application from Labour MP Helen Hayes. The neutral motion was held without a division. Windrush Day, marked nationally on June 22, commemorates the 1948 arrival of HMT Empire Windrush at Tilbury Docks, when the ship brought 1,027 passengers — over 800 from the Caribbean — who held Citizen of the UK and Colonies status under the British Nationality Act 1948.

The day carries a sharper edge because of the Windrush scandal, the political crisis that broke in 2018 when long-settled Caribbean migrants who arrived before 1973 were wrongly treated as illegal immigrants, denied healthcare and employment, detained and, in at least 83 cases, deported. The Windrush Compensation Scheme, launched in April 2019, had paid out £127 million across nearly 4,000 claims as of March 2026, though campaigners say too many victims have died waiting and that the process remains too slow.
