### UK Pound Slides to 1.3195 Against Dollar as Flash PMI Hits 14-Month Low Amid Political Transition

Sterling traded at 1.3195 against the dollar in early European dealings on June 24, with selling pressure persisting below the 1.3200 level. The pound was squeezed from two directions at once. At home, the UK's flash S&P Global Composite PMI for June fell to 49.4, a 14-month low and down from 49.7 in May, marking a second straight month of private-sector contraction as services activity weakened. Manufacturing softened too, with the PMI easing to 53.1 from 53.9. The 50 line separates growth from contraction, so a sub-50 composite reading signals the broader economy is shrinking.

Abroad, the dollar drew strength from a hawkish Federal Reserve. The market-implied probability of a December rate hike jumped to 86.1%, up from 61% before the latest FOMC meeting. "The dollar's strength right now, at the end of the day, it's still hawkishness," said Eugene Epstein, Head of Trading and Structured Products at Moneycorp.

Layered on top is acute political uncertainty. Prime Minister Keir Starmer resigned on 22 June, following Andy Burnham's victory in the Makerfield by-election that returned him to Parliament. Traders are now scrutinising what a Burnham-led government would mean for the public finances. "Markets will be focused on Burnham's views on fiscal policy and whether there will be any relaxation of the current fiscal rules. A loosening in fiscal rules would likely be poorly received by the UK bond market," said Commonwealth Bank of Australia strategist Kristina Clifton.

## 2. Politics & Diplomacy

### Andy Burnham Frontrunner for UK Prime Minister as Labour Leadership Race Opens After Starmer Resignation

Andy Burnham is the frontrunner to become the UK's next prime minister, following Keir Starmer's resignation announcement on 22 June 2026. Mayor of Greater Manchester since 2017, Burnham has built his pitch around what he calls "Manchesterism" — a devolution-driven model that prizes long-term planning and local control over Whitehall centralisation, exemplified by initiatives such as the publicly controlled Bee Network bus system. He casts it as a "modern and functional response" to the low-growth trap left by decades of overcentralised Treasury power.

The path is unusually clear. Wes Streeting, previously seen as his main rival, endorsed him on 22 June. Senior cabinet figures lined up behind Burnham as of 23 June, including Deputy PM David Lammy, Education Secretary Bridget Phillipson and Scotland Secretary Douglas Alexander. Under Labour rules a candidate needs the support of at least 81 MPs — 20% of the parliamentary party — to stand, a threshold that caps the field at five contenders. Nominations open 9 July and close 16 July. If unopposed, Burnham could become prime minister by 17 July; if a contest occurs, the result would land by 1 September.

A Bloomberg Opinion piece published 24 June pressed a sceptical case against scaling up his record nationally, arguing that Greater Manchester's outperformance against the national average is partly an artefact of administrative boundaries — flattering metrics that capture a prosperous core — rather than a blueprint readily transferable to the whole country.

## 4. Society & Livelihood

### UK Met Office Issues Only Second-Ever Red Extreme Heat Warning as June Records Forecast to Break

The Met Office issued a Red Extreme Heat Warning for England and Wales on 24 June 2026 — only the second in its history, the first having been issued in July 2022 when temperatures exceeded 40°C. The warning is in force from 09:00 Wednesday 24 June through 21:00 Thursday 25 June, covering six regions: the West Midlands, East Midlands, South East, South West, London and the East of England, with amber alerts extended across the North West, North East, and Yorkshire and the Humber. Peak temperatures are forecast at 37°C on Wednesday and 38°C on Thursday and Friday.

Records are set to topple. The UK's all-time June record of 35.6°C — set in Southampton in 1976 and at Camden Square in 1957 — is very likely to be exceeded, while Wales's June record of 33.7°C, set in 2000, is also forecast to break; Wales already recorded 20.3°C overnight on Monday, with dew points reaching roughly 22°C making the air feel oppressively humid. "Red warnings are reserved for the most severe events and we're expecting severe and significant impacts from this heatwave, with health impacts likely for many, even beyond those who are normally more vulnerable to the heat," said Met Office Deputy Chief Forecaster Mark Sidaway. Chief Scientist Stephen Belcher added: "Human induced climate change has made events like this more likely and more intense."

Disruption is already widespread. Over 850 schools across England and Wales closed or finished early, Eurostar cancelled four London-Paris trains, Chiltern Railways axed more than half its services, and the British Army cancelled the Changing of the Guard at Buckingham Palace. The UKHSA issued a corresponding red heat-health alert. "A red heat health alert indicates a risk to life for even the healthy population, but simple actions like staying hydrated, avoiding the sun during the hottest part of the day, and keeping your home cool can make a big difference," said Dr Agostinho Sousa, UKHSA Head of Extreme Events. A Yellow Thunderstorm Warning follows Thursday evening with gusts potentially reaching 50 mph. The same heat dome pushed France to its hottest-ever national day and knocked out power to tens of thousands of homes in the country's west, with conditions expected to ease in Britain by Friday.

### Climate Change Committee Lays 2026 Emissions Progress Report Before Parliament, UK Still 64 MtCO₂e Short of 2030 Target

UK greenhouse gas emissions reached 407 MtCO₂e in 2025, representing a 50% reduction below 1990 levels — the first time the halfway milestone has been crossed — according to the Climate Change Committee's statutory annual progress report, laid before Parliament on 24 June 2026 as required under the Climate Change Act 2008. Emissions fell 1.8% year-on-year, down 7.3 MtCO₂e from 2024.

The picture is one of progress shadowed by a widening shortfall. The Fourth Carbon Budget, covering 2023 to 2027, is now highly likely to be achieved. But the current pathway falls short of the UK's 2030 Nationally Determined Contribution by approximately 64 MtCO₂e — a gap 11 MtCO₂e larger than noted in last year's assessment, implying the pace of cuts now needs to almost double. Bright spots cluster in transport and heating: electric vehicles on the road surpassed 2 million for the first time as of April 2026, EVs were 24% of new car sales in 2025, and heat pump installations reached 52,000 units. The report, prepared by CCC staff including Emma Pinchbeck, James Richardson, Emily Nurse, Eoin Devane, Esther Harris and Sam Karslake with expert advisor Professor Rebecca Willis, notes the UK accounts for less than 1% of global emissions. The deadline for legislating the Seventh Carbon Budget is 30 June 2026.

### SEGRO Rejects £12.6 Billion All-Share Takeover Approach from US Logistics Giant Prologis

US-based logistics and industrial real estate company Prologis, Inc. publicly disclosed on 24 June 2026 an indicative all-share proposal to acquire SEGRO plc, the FTSE 100 industrial and logistics REIT, valuing SEGRO at approximately £12.6 billion. The proposal was communicated privately to SEGRO's board on 16 June 2026; the board unequivocally rejected it on 23 June, prompting Prologis to take its case public the next day.

The terms offered 0.084 new Prologis shares for each SEGRO share, implying a value of 925 pence per share. That marked a 24.6% premium to SEGRO's closing price of 742 pence on 23 June, a 26.7% premium to the one-month volume-weighted average price of 730 pence, and a 31.4% premium to the three-month VWAP of 704 pence. SEGRO shareholders would have ended up holding roughly 10.5% of the enlarged Prologis. Prologis argued the tie-up would unlock the embedded value of SEGRO's development and data centre pipeline — a strategic prize as warehouse landlords race to repurpose land and power capacity for AI-driven computing demand — in a way SEGRO could not achieve standalone.

Under UK Takeover Code Rule 2.6(a), Prologis must announce a firm intention to make an offer, or walk away, by 22 July 2026 at 17:00 London time. Rothschild & Co and J.P. Morgan Securities are advising Prologis, with Linklaters LLP acting as legal counsel.

### Ratio Petroleum to Acquire Pharos Energy via £124.3 Million Cash Scheme of Arrangement

Ratio Petroleum Energy LP announced on 24 June 2026 a recommended acquisition of Pharos Energy plc through a court-approved scheme of arrangement under Part 26 of the Companies Act 2006. The offer is up to 28 pence per share in cash, comprising 23.0683 pence cash consideration, a 4.0 pence special dividend and a 0.9317 pence FY25 final dividend. The structure values Pharos at approximately £124.3 million, a 10.24% premium to its closing price of 25.4 pence on 23 June 2026.

Ratio has already secured irrevocable undertakings representing approximately 41.76% of Pharos's issued share capital, lending the deal momentum. Pharos, the London-listed independent formerly known as SOCO International, runs producing oil and gas assets in Vietnam and Egypt; its 2025 production was 5,398 barrels of oil equivalent per day with revenues of $114.6 million. "Over the last 23 months, the team has put Pharos in a position where this all-cash offer is possible," said Pharos CEO Katherine Roe. Ratio CEO Itay Raphael said: "We are pleased to announce our recommended offer for Pharos today, creates a balanced production and exploration company." The expected effective date is H1 2027, pending court approval, with Shore Capital advising Ratio and Rothschild & Co advising Pharos.
