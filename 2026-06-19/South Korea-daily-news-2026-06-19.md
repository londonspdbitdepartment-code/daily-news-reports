### KOSPI tops 9,000 for the first time as chip rally lifts SK Hynix past W2,000 trillion market cap

The KOSPI opened June 19 at 9,288.89, up 225.05 points or 2.48 percent, the first time the benchmark had ever traded above 9,000. By 9:04 a.m. KST it reached 9,331.55, clearing the previous intraday record of 9,106.07 set only the day before, on June 18. The index has now roughly doubled this year, riding an AI-memory boom that has made Korean chipmakers among the best-performing large-cap stocks anywhere in 2026.

The trigger overnight was U.S. President Trump's announcement that Intel had secured an agreement with Apple for domestic chip design and manufacturing, which lifted semiconductor sentiment globally. SK Hynix advanced 2.8% to ₩132,000 and became the second Korean company ever to pass 2,000 trillion won in market value, touching 2.038 trillion won (about USD 1.32 trillion) intraday. Samsung Electronics, up 3.1% to ₩89,500, held first place at roughly 2.18 trillion won. The two chipmakers alone now account for roughly 52 percent of total KOSPI market capitalisation — a concentration that has climbed steadily through the year — and the exchange's combined value passed 8,000 trillion won for the first time.

Retail investors were net buyers; foreign and institutional investors sold. An analyst from Kiwoom Securities cautioned that "the rise could be limited due to profit taking." The smaller-cap KOSDAQ opened at 1,001.40 but slipped back below the 1,000-point line.

### Hotel Shilla shares climb 30% in three months as record tourist arrivals revive duty-free business

Hotel Shilla Co.'s stock has gained 30 percent over three months, driven by rising hotel occupancy and a turnaround in its long-struggling duty-free arm. The recovery rides a record inbound-tourism wave: foreign arrivals reached 18.9 million in 2025, a 15.7 percent jump and an all-time high, propelled by the global pull of Korean culture. The spending those visitors bring flows straight to operators like Shilla, which runs both luxury hotels and duty-free retail — the segment that bled cash during the post-pandemic slump in Chinese group tourism.

The rally tracks a policy push from the top. President Lee Jae Myung convened an Expanded National Tourism Strategy Meeting with Hotel Shilla CEO Lee Boo-jin, who also chairs the Visit Korea Committee, to unveil a roadmap targeting 30 million foreign visitors by 2030. Lee has framed tourism as the commercial payoff of the K-wave, arguing that the cultural industry must "ultimately lead to tourism," with reforms to immigration, regional airports and lodging meant to spread arrivals beyond Seoul.

### Government moves to dismiss heads of two state agencies after performance review

South Korea's Ministry of Finance and Economy will seek the removal of the heads of two public institutions — the Government Employees Pension Service and the Korea International Cooperation Agency (KOICA) — after performance evaluations by the Ownership Steering Committee. The committee graded 88 state-run organisations and 82 agency leaders on a six-tier system running A through E. Seven institutions landed the lowest grade, E.

The fallout reached further than the two dismissals. Seventeen agency heads received D grades and formal warnings, and the leaders of 11 organisations — among them Korea Electric Power Corp. (KEPCO) and Korea Gas Corp. — were issued cautions following fatal workplace accidents. The ministry said its criteria weighed "social responsibility in areas such as safety and green initiatives, as well as financial health and productivity," and that organisations showing effective use of artificial intelligence were viewed favourably.

## 2. Politics & Diplomacy

### Election watchdog panel urges criminal probe of ex-chairman over ballot-shortage 'total failure'

A fact-finding committee at the National Election Commission (NEC) labelled the management of the June 3 local and by-elections a "total failure" and called for criminal proceedings against former NEC Chairman Roh Tae-ak, who had already resigned over the episode. The six-member panel — drawn from civic groups, media, the legal profession and academia — was launched June 10 and presented its conclusions June 19 at NEC headquarters in Gwacheon, Gyeonggi Province.

The shortages hit 50 polling stations and temporarily halted voting at 22 sites nationwide on election day. The disruption was sharpest in Seoul's Songpa District; protesters gathered to demand a rerun, and Roh resigned on June 5, apologising for an administrative breakdown that infringed on voting rights. Committee chair Cho Hyun-wook went further than personnel changes, recommending a restructuring "tantamount to dismantling and rebuilding the organisation."

### Ex-Defense Minister Kim Yong-hyun gets three years for leaking military secrets in martial-law plot

The Seoul Central District Court sentenced former Defense Minister Kim Yong-hyun to three years in prison for handing a list of military intelligence personnel to Noh Sang-won, a former Defense Intelligence Command chief who had been dishonourably discharged six years earlier. The court found Kim leaked the data between October and November 2024 despite his duty "to protect military secrets and personal information of service members," with then-Defense Intelligence Commander Moon Sang-ho among the co-conspirators.

The court tied the breach directly to the wider conspiracy, calling Kim's actions a "driving force" behind former President Yoon Suk Yeol's December 2024 martial law declaration. Prosecutors alleged the personnel list was meant to staff a "second investigation unit" under martial law to pursue unsubstantiated election-fraud claims. Yoon himself was convicted of insurrection and handed a life sentence in February 2026 — a verdict he has appealed. Kim's lawyer said he too will appeal, arguing the leaked information had never been formally designated a military secret.

### Assembly Speaker presses foreign minister to bring home 24 ships stranded near Hormuz

National Assembly Speaker Cho Jeong-sik met Foreign Minister Cho Hyun on June 19 over 24 South Korean-linked vessels still stuck in ports across Qatar and the United Arab Emirates, with 137 Korean sailors aboard. The ships have been unable to move since commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz halted during the U.S.-Iran war. A ceasefire ended 106 days of fighting, but the strait has not fully reopened to commercial shipping.

"I urge you to take all possible diplomatic measures to ensure that the 24 vessels and their crew members stranded in the Strait of Hormuz can return home safely," Cho told the minister. He pledged parliamentary backing for what he called pragmatic diplomacy, and the foreign ministry said it has opened consultations with the U.S., Iran and other governments on safe passage.

## 3. Technology & Industry

### Science ministry signs AI-safety MOU with Anthropic days after U.S. blocks Claude access in Korea

South Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT signed a memorandum of understanding with Anthropic on June 18 to deepen cooperation on AI safety and cybersecurity, with the work to cover AI's effect on cyber offence and defence, the evaluation of model safety in Korean-language settings, and red-team testing of autonomous AI agents. The deal traces back to talks between Deputy Prime Minister Bae Kyung-hoon and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei at the February 2026 AI Action Summit in India. "Korea is one of Anthropic's most important markets in the Asia-Pacific region," said Chris Ciauri, the company's Managing Director of International. Korea ranks 12th globally in per-capita Claude usage and is expected to enter Anthropic's top 10 markets.

The signing comes against a sharp diplomatic backdrop. On June 12, U.S. export controls cut off foreign nationals' access to Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5 and Fable 5 models after the White House learned that SK Telecom — described as a Korean telecom with alleged ties to China — had reached Mythos through Project Glasswing, the company's invite-only cybersecurity consortium. SK Telecom denied the allegations. Roughly 150 organisations held Glasswing access, including Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix and KISA. Anthropic opened its Seoul office — its fourth in Asia — alongside the MOU and unveiled a slate of enterprise Claude deployments with Samsung SDS, LG CNS, Nexon and Hanwha Solutions.

## 4. Society & Livelihood

### Health insurance to cover severe-alopecia drug Olumiant from July 1, reigniting fight over spending priorities

The National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) will begin covering Olumiant tablets (baricitinib) for severe alopecia areata on July 1, 2026, at an estimated 100 billion won a year. Eligibility is tightly drawn: a SALT score of at least 50 points — or 20-49 with severe visible loss such as complete eyebrow loss — documented failure of three months of alternative treatments, and a two-year coverage cap with reviews at 36 weeks and every six months thereafter.

The decision, championed by President Lee Jae Myung, drew immediate criticism over where the money should go. The Korea Federation for Severe Disease Patients called it "a populist policy that undermines the foundation of the NHIS," noting that critically ill patients with rare diseases still lack coverage for costly treatments. One expert warned, "Once you abandon medical necessity as the standard for insurance coverage, policy priorities risk being driven by whichever interest groups have the loudest voices." The dispute carries weight because the system's finances are tightening — NHIS reserves stood at 30.2 trillion won in 2025 but are projected to run dry by 2033 as the population ages and costs climb.

### Rights commission tells Justice Ministry to let immigration detainees get hospital surgery

The National Human Rights Commission recommended that Justice Minister Jung Sung-ho allow immigration detainees access to outside hospitals for needed surgery, after two complaints filed in January 2026 — one detainee required an operation for a torn medial meniscus, the other for multiple fractures. The commission found that denying surgical care at external facilities "amounts to a human rights violation," stressing that immigration detention restricts liberty without independent judicial oversight.

The detention centre argued that outside medical visits require approval based on injury severity and flight risk. The commission rejected that calculus where health is at stake, concluding that detainees in "ongoing pain" must receive "appropriate surgical care at outside hospitals."

## 5. Corporate IPO & M&A

### Kurly revives IPO plan after first sustained quarterly profit and an AI acquisition

Kurly, South Korea's major online grocery platform, posted Q1 2026 revenue of 745.7 billion won, up 28.4% year-on-year, and record operating profit of 24.2 billion won — a 13-fold jump that marks its first sustained quarterly profitability and sheds the loss-making label that long shadowed its valuation. The financial repair runs deep: the interest coverage ratio improved from 0.23x to 3.31x, financial costs fell from 7.7 billion to 7.3 billion won, and inventory turnover rose to 24.4 times from 23.1.

The turnaround clears the path back to an initial public offering. Kurly's current valuation sits at roughly 2.8 trillion won (about USD 1.9 billion), and Naver lifted its stake from 5.08% to 6.19% through a capital injection, bringing the combined holding of CEO Kim Slah and Naver to about 11.9%. To bolster its growth story ahead of listing, Kurly is acquiring AI solutions firm Wonji Labs as a wholly-owned subsidiary. Priced against that 2.8 trillion won valuation, the IPO would comfortably clear the USD 300 million primary-band threshold.

### Trump asks Korea to build 10 warships for U.S. Navy as Hanwha Ocean chases CAD$39B Canadian submarine deal

President Lee Jae Myung said that at the G7 summit in France, U.S. President Donald Trump asked whether Seoul could rapidly build 10 warships for the American Navy, and that he assured Trump Korea was capable. Hanwha has spent months building out the bid, formalising industrial agreements with Canadian firms and docking a KSS-III submarine in British Columbia to showcase the platform. Lee tempered expectations: "We have high hopes based on our overall assessment, but it is not so easy as to be confident."
